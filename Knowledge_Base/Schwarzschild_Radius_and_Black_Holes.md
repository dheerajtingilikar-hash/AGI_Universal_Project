# Schwarzschild Radius and Black Holes
## Overview
The [[Schwarzschild Radius]] is a fundamental concept in [[Astrophysics]] and [[General Relativity]], describing the radius of a [[Black Hole]] within which nothing, including [[Light]], can escape the gravitational pull. This radius is named after [[Karl Schwarzschild]], who first proposed it in 1916. The Schwarzschild Radius is directly proportional to the [[Mass]] of the black hole and is a key factor in understanding the behavior of these cosmic phenomena.

## Concept Map
- [[Black Holes]]: Regions in space where the gravitational pull is so strong that nothing can escape.
- [[Event Horizon]]: The boundary beyond which nothing, including light, can escape the gravitational pull of a black hole. The event horizon is located at the Schwarzschild Radius.
- [[Singularity]]: A point within a black hole where the density and curvature of space-time are infinite.
- [[Gravitational Pull]]: The force exerted by a massive object, such as a black hole, on other objects.
- [[General Relativity]]: The theory of gravitation developed by [[Albert Einstein]], which describes gravity as the curvature of [[Space-Time]] caused by mass and energy.
- [[Astrophysics]]: The branch of [[Astronomy]] that deals with the physical nature of celestial objects and phenomena.
- [[Karl Schwarzschild]]: A German physicist who made significant contributions to the understanding of black holes and the development of general relativity.
