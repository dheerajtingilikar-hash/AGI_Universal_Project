# Stereochemistry
## Overview
Stereochemistry is the branch of [[Chemistry]] that deals with the three-dimensional arrangement of atoms in [[Molecule]]s. It is a crucial aspect of understanding the properties and behavior of molecules, as the spatial arrangement of atoms can significantly impact their [[Reactivity]] and [[Physical Properties]]. Stereochemistry is essential in various fields, including [[Pharmaceutical]]s, [[Biochemistry]], and [[Materials Science]].

## Concept Map
The key concepts in stereochemistry include:
* [[Isomer]]s: molecules with the same molecular formula but different three-dimensional arrangements of atoms
* [[Enantiomer]]s: non-superimposable mirror images of each other
* [[Diastereomer]]s: stereoisomers that are not enantiomers
* [[Chirality]]: the property of a molecule that makes it non-superimposable on its mirror image
* [[R/S Configuration]]: a system used to describe the stereochemistry of a molecule
* [[Fischer Projection]]: a two-dimensional representation of a molecule used to describe its stereochemistry

## Key Principles
The key principles of stereochemistry include:
* The [[Tetrahedral]] arrangement of atoms in a molecule
* The concept of [[Steric Hindrance]] and its impact on molecular shape
* The importance of [[Molecular Symmetry]] in determining the properties of a molecule

## Applications
Stereochemistry has numerous applications in various fields, including:
* [[Drug Design]]: understanding the stereochemistry of a molecule is crucial in designing effective drugs
* [[Catalysis]]: stereochemistry plays a key role in understanding the mechanism of catalytic reactions
* [[Materials Science]]: stereochemistry is essential in understanding the properties of materials and designing new materials with specific properties.
