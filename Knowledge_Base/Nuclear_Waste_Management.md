# Nuclear Waste Management

## Overview

Nuclear waste management refers to the practices and strategies employed to handle and dispose of radioactive materials generated by nuclear power plants, medical facilities, and other sources. The primary goal of nuclear waste management is to minimize the risks associated with radioactive waste, including environmental contamination, human exposure, and long-term health effects.

### Types of Nuclear Waste

- **Low-Level Waste (LLW)**: Radioactive waste with low levels of radioactivity, typically generated from medical and industrial applications.
- **High-Level Waste (HLW)**: Radioactive waste with high levels of radioactivity, typically generated from nuclear power plants and reprocessing facilities.
- **Transuranic Waste (TRU)**: Radioactive waste containing transuranic elements, typically generated from nuclear reactors and fuel reprocessing.

## Storage and Disposal Methods

- **Dry Cask Storage**: A method of storing spent nuclear fuel in a dry, airtight container.
- **Wet Storage**: A method of storing spent nuclear fuel in a pool of water.
- **Geologic Disposal**: A method of disposing of nuclear waste in a deep geological repository.
- **Vitrification**: A method of converting nuclear waste into a stable glass form.

## Regulations and Guidelines

- **Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC)**: Regulates nuclear waste management in the United States.
- **International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA)**: Develops guidelines and standards for nuclear waste management worldwide.
- **European Union's (EU) Nuclear Waste Directive**: Regulates nuclear waste management in EU member states.
