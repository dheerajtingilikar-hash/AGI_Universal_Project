# Semiconductor Physics
## Overview
Semiconductor physics is the study of the physical properties and behavior of [[Semiconductor]] materials. These materials have electrical conductivity between that of a [[Conductor]] and an [[Insulator]], making them crucial for the development of modern electronic devices. The field of semiconductor physics involves understanding the behavior of [[Electron]]s and [[Hole (physics)|holes]] in these materials, as well as the effects of [[Doping (semiconductor)|doping]] and other external factors.

## Concept Map
The key concepts in semiconductor physics can be organized into the following categories:
* [[Crystal Structure]]: The arrangement of atoms in a semiconductor material, which affects its electronic properties.
* [[Energy Band]]: The range of energies that an electron can occupy in a semiconductor material.
* [[Fermi Level]]: The energy level at which the probability of finding an electron is 50%.
* [[Carrier Mobility]]: The ability of electrons and holes to move through a semiconductor material.
* [[Doping (semiconductor)|Doping]]: The introduction of impurities into a semiconductor material to alter its electronic properties.
* [[PN Junction]]: The interface between two semiconductor materials with different doping levels, which is the basis for many electronic devices.

## Key Equations
The behavior of semiconductors can be described using the following equations:
* [[Drift-Diffusion Equation]]: Describes the movement of charge carriers in a semiconductor material.
* [[Shockley Diode Equation]]: Describes the current-voltage characteristics of a [[PN Junction]].

## Applications
Semiconductor physics has numerous applications in modern technology, including:
* [[Transistor]]s: Amplify or switch electronic signals.
* [[Diode]]s: Control the flow of electric current.
* [[Solar Cell]]s: Convert light into electrical energy.
* [[Integrated Circuit]]s: Combine multiple electronic components into a single device.
