# Unitary vs Federated State Administrative Distribution

## Overview

[[Administrative division]], [[Government structure]], and [[State organization]] are all related concepts that describe how power is distributed within a [[State (polity)]]. Two primary models of state administration are the unitary and federated systems.

### Unitary State

In a unitary state, the central government holds supreme authority and control over all aspects of governance, including [[Local government]] and [[Regional government]]. This system is characterized by a centralized power structure, where decisions are made at the national level and implemented uniformly across the country.

### Federated State

A federated state, also known as a [[Federal state]], is a system where power is divided between a central government and constituent units, such as states or provinces. Each constituent unit has a degree of autonomy and is responsible for administering its own affairs, while the central government retains authority over matters of national importance.
