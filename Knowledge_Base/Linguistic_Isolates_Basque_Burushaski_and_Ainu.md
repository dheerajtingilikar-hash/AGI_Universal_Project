# Linguistic Isolates: Basque, Burushaski, and Ainu

## Overview

Linguistic isolates are languages that do not belong to any known language family. This entry focuses on three notable linguistic isolates: Basque, Burushaski, and Ainu.

### Basque

- **Language Family:** Linguistic isolate
- **Native Speakers:** 600,000 (Basque Country and Navarre, Spain; Labourd, Soule, and Lower Navarre, France)
- **Language Code:** eu
- **Classification:** Agglutinative language
- **Geographical Distribution:** Basque Country and Navarre, Spain; Labourd, Soule, and Lower Navarre, France

### Burushaski

- **Language Family:** Linguistic isolate
- **Native Speakers:** 100,000 (Hunza Valley, Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan)
- **Language Code:** bsy
- **Classification:** Agglutinative language
- **Geographical Distribution:** Hunza Valley, Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan

### Ainu

- **Language Family:** Linguistic isolate
- **Native Speakers:** 10 (mainly elderly individuals, Hokkaido, Japan)
- **Language Code:** ain
- **Classification:** Agglutinative language
- **Geographical Distribution:** Hokkaido, Japan
