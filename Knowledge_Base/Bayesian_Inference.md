# Bayesian Inference
Bayesian inference is a statistical framework for updating the probability estimate for a hypothesis as more [[Evidence]] or [[Information]] becomes available. It is based on [[Bayes' Theorem]], which describes the [[Probability]] of an event based on prior knowledge and new evidence.

## Overview
Bayesian inference is a method of [[Statistical Inference]] that involves updating the [[Probability Distribution]] of a hypothesis based on new [[Data]]. It provides a mathematical framework for updating the probability of a hypothesis as more information becomes available. The process involves specifying a [[Prior Distribution]] for the hypothesis, which is then updated using [[Likelihood]] and [[Posterior Distribution]].

## Concept Map
The key concepts in Bayesian inference are:
* [[Prior Distribution]]: the initial probability distribution of the hypothesis
* [[Likelihood]]: the probability of observing the data given the hypothesis
* [[Posterior Distribution]]: the updated probability distribution of the hypothesis after observing the data
* [[Bayes' Theorem]]: the mathematical formula for updating the probability of a hypothesis based on new evidence
* [[Markov Chain Monte Carlo]]: a computational method for approximating the posterior distribution
* [[Maximum A Posteriori]]: a method for estimating the most likely value of the hypothesis given the data

## Applications
Bayesian inference has a wide range of applications in [[Machine Learning]], [[Artificial Intelligence]], [[Signal Processing]], and [[Statistics]]. It is used in [[Image Processing]], [[Natural Language Processing]], and [[Recommendation Systems]]. Bayesian inference is also used in [[Medical Diagnosis]], [[Financial Modeling]], and [[Climate Modeling]].
