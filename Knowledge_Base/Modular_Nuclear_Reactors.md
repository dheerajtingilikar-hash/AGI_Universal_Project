# Modular Nuclear Reactors

## Overview

Modular nuclear reactors are a type of nuclear fission reactor designed to be manufactured and assembled in a factory, then transported and installed at a power plant site. This approach offers several advantages, including improved safety, reduced construction time, and increased efficiency.

### Characteristics

- **Modularity**: Modular reactors are designed to be composed of multiple, self-contained modules that can be easily assembled and disassembled.
- **Factory assembly**: The reactor is assembled in a factory, reducing the risk of on-site construction errors and improving quality control.
- **Transportability**: The reactor modules are designed to be transported by truck or ship, reducing the need for on-site construction and minimizing the impact on the surrounding environment.
- **Scalability**: Modular reactors can be easily scaled up or down to meet changing energy demands.

### Types

- **Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)**: SMRs are a type of modular reactor that is designed to be smaller than traditional nuclear reactors, with a power output of less than 300 MWe.
- **Integral Pressurized Water Reactors (iPWRs)**: iPWRs are a type of modular reactor that combines the reactor vessel, steam generator, and pressurizer into a single, self-contained module.
- **High-Temperature Gas Reactors (HTGRs)**: HTGRs are a type of modular reactor that uses a graphite core and operates at high temperatures, making it suitable for process heat applications.
