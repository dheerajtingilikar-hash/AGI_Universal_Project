# Vertical Take-Off and Landing (VTOL)

## Overview

Vertical Take-Off and Landing (VTOL) refers to the ability of an aircraft to take off and land vertically, without the need for a runway. This capability is achieved through the use of lift fans, rotors, or other propulsion systems that allow the aircraft to generate lift and thrust in a vertical direction.

### Characteristics

- **Lift**: VTOL aircraft generate lift through the use of rotors, fans, or other lift-generating devices.
- **Thrust**: VTOL aircraft generate thrust through the use of engines, fans, or other propulsion systems.
- **Control**: VTOL aircraft use a combination of control surfaces and propulsion systems to maintain stability and control during takeoff, landing, and flight.

### Applications

- **Military**: VTOL aircraft are used for military operations, such as search and rescue, medical evacuation, and combat.
- **Civilian**: VTOL aircraft are used for civilian applications, such as air taxis, medical transport, and cargo transport.

### Examples

- **Harrier Jump Jet**: A British military VTOL aircraft that uses a lift fan to generate lift and thrust.
- **Osprey**: A US military VTOL aircraft that uses tiltrotors to generate lift and thrust.
- **Lilium Jet**: A German civilian VTOL aircraft that uses electric fans to generate lift and thrust.
