# Sign Language Isolates and Non-Verbal Syntax

## Overview

Sign language isolates are sign languages that do not have a known linguistic relationship with any other sign language or spoken language. They often exhibit unique grammatical and phonological structures. Non-verbal syntax refers to the rules governing the arrangement of signs and other non-verbal elements in a sign language.

### Definition

Sign language isolates are typically characterized by:

* Lack of linguistic relationship with other sign languages or spoken languages
* Unique grammatical and phonological structures
* Often have a distinct vocabulary and syntax

### Examples

* Nicaraguan Sign Language (NSL)
* Al-Sayyid Bedouin Sign Language (ABSL)
* Kata Kolok

## Linguistic Characteristics

### Sign Order

Sign order refers to the arrangement of signs in a sign language. In some sign languages, signs are arranged in a linear sequence, while in others, signs are arranged in a more complex spatial arrangement.

### Iconicity

Iconicity refers to the degree to which a sign resembles the thing or action it represents. Iconic signs are often more memorable and easier to learn.

### Topology

Topology refers to the spatial arrangement of signs in a sign language. In some sign languages, signs are arranged in a two-dimensional space, while in others, signs are arranged in a three-dimensional space.
