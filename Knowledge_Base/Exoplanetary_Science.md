# Exoplanetary Science
Exoplanetary science is the study of [[Exoplanet]]s, which are [[Planet]]s that orbit stars outside of our own [[Solar System]]. This field of research has grown rapidly in recent years, with thousands of exoplanets discovered using a variety of detection methods.

## Overview
Exoplanetary science is an interdisciplinary field that combines [[Astronomy]], [[Astrophysics]], and [[Planetary Science]] to study the formation, composition, and properties of exoplanets. The discovery of exoplanets has revolutionized our understanding of [[Planet Formation]] and the potential for [[Life]] beyond [[Earth]]. Exoplanetary scientists use a range of techniques, including [[Transit Method]] and [[Radial Velocity Method]], to detect and characterize exoplanets.

## Concept Map
The study of exoplanetary science involves several key concepts, including:
* [[Exoplanet Classification]]: the categorization of exoplanets based on their size, mass, and composition
* [[Habitable Zone]]: the region around a star where temperatures are suitable for [[Liquid Water]] to exist
* [[Atmospheric Escape]]: the loss of atmospheric gases from an exoplanet due to [[Solar Wind]] or other processes
* [[Biosignature]]: signs of biological activity, such as the presence of [[Oxygen]] or [[Methane]], in an exoplanet's atmosphere
* [[Exoplanet Atmosphere]]: the layer of gases surrounding an exoplanet, which can provide clues about its composition and potential habitability
