# Generative Grammar
Generative grammar is a [[Linguistics]] theory that attempts to explain how [[Language]] is generated and understood by the human mind.

## Overview
Generative grammar is based on the idea that language is a [[Cognitive]] system that consists of a set of rules and principles that generate an infinite number of possible [[Sentence]]s from a finite set of [[Word]]s. This theory was first proposed by [[Noam Chomsky]] in the 1950s and has since become a major area of research in [[Linguistics]]. The core idea of generative grammar is that language is a [[Mental]] construct, and that the rules and principles that govern language are innate to the human mind.

## Concept Map
The key concepts in generative grammar include:
* [[Phonology]]: the study of the sound system of language
* [[Morphology]]: the study of the internal structure of words
* [[Syntax]]: the study of the rules that govern the formation of sentences
* [[Semantics]]: the study of the meaning of language
* [[Pragmatics]]: the study of how language is used in context
* [[Universal Grammar]]: the idea that all human languages share a common underlying structure
* [[Poverty of the Stimulus]]: the idea that the input that children receive is insufficient to explain their ability to acquire language
