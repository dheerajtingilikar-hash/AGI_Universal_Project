# Laws of Thermodynamics
The Laws of Thermodynamics are a set of fundamental principles that describe the relationships between [[Heat]], [[Work]], and [[Energy]].

## Overview
The Laws of Thermodynamics are a cornerstone of [[Physics]] and [[Chemistry]], providing a framework for understanding the behavior of [[Energy]] and its interactions with [[Matter]]. The laws are based on empirical observations and have been widely accepted as a fundamental theory. The four laws of thermodynamics are: 
1. The [[Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics]], which defines [[Temperature]].
2. The [[First Law of Thermodynamics]], which describes the conservation of [[Energy]].
3. The [[Second Law of Thermodynamics]], which describes the direction of [[Spontaneous Process|spontaneous processes]].
4. The [[Third Law of Thermodynamics]], which describes the behavior of [[Entropy]] at absolute [[Zero]].

## Concept Map
The Laws of Thermodynamics can be related to other concepts in [[Physics]] and [[Chemistry]] as follows:
* [[Thermodynamic System|Thermodynamic systems]]: The Laws of Thermodynamics apply to [[Thermodynamic System|thermodynamic systems]], which are systems that exchange [[Energy]] and [[Matter]] with their surroundings.
* [[Thermodynamic Process|Thermodynamic processes]]: The Laws of Thermodynamics describe the behavior of [[Thermodynamic Process|thermodynamic processes]], such as [[Isothermal Process|isothermal]] and [[Adiabatic Process|adiabatic]] processes.
* [[Equation of State|Equations of state]]: The Laws of Thermodynamics are related to [[Equation of State|equations of state]], which describe the relationships between [[Thermodynamic Variable|thermodynamic variables]] such as [[Pressure]], [[Volume]], and [[Temperature]].
* [[Statistical Mechanics|Statistical mechanics]]: The Laws of Thermodynamics can be derived from [[Statistical Mechanics|statistical mechanics]], which provides a microscopic understanding of the behavior of [[Matter]] and [[Energy]].
