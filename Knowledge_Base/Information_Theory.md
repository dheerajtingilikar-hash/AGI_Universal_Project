# Information Theory
Information theory is a branch of [[Mathematics]] that deals with the quantification, storage, and communication of [[Information]]. It was developed by [[Claude Shannon]] in the 1940s and has since become a fundamental theory in [[Computer Science]], [[Engineering]], and [[Cryptography]].

## Overview
Information theory provides a mathematical framework for understanding the fundamental limits of [[Data Compression]], [[Data Transmission]], and [[Error Correction]]. It introduces concepts such as [[Entropy (Information Theory)|Entropy]], [[Mutual Information]], and [[Channel Capacity]], which are essential for designing and optimizing [[Communication Systems]].

## Concept Map
* [[Entropy (Information Theory)|Entropy]]: a measure of the uncertainty or randomness of a [[Probability Distribution]]
* [[Mutual Information]]: a measure of the dependence between two [[Random Variable|Random Variables]]
* [[Channel Capacity]]: the maximum rate at which [[Information]] can be reliably transmitted over a [[Communication Channel]]
* [[Data Compression]]: the process of reducing the size of a [[Data Set]] while preserving its [[Information Content]]
* [[Error Correction]]: the process of detecting and correcting [[Error|Errors]] that occur during [[Data Transmission]]
* [[Coding Theory]]: the study of [[Error-Correcting Code|Error-Correcting Codes]] and their applications in [[Data Transmission]] and [[Data Storage]]
