# General Relativity
General Relativity is a fundamental concept in [[Physics]] that describes the nature of [[Gravity]] and its effects on [[Spacetime]].

## Overview
General Relativity is a [[Theory]] developed by [[Albert Einstein]] that revolutionized our understanding of the universe. It postulates that [[Gravity]] is not a force, but rather a curvature of [[Spacetime]] caused by the presence of [[Mass]] and [[Energy]]. The theory is based on the [[Equivalence Principle]], which states that all objects fall at the same rate in a [[Gravitational Field]], regardless of their composition or structure.

## Concept Map
The key concepts in General Relativity include:
* [[Spacetime]]: a four-dimensional [[Manifold]] that combines [[Space]] and [[Time]]
* [[Metric Tensor]]: a mathematical object that describes the curvature of [[Spacetime]]
* [[Geodesic Equation]]: a mathematical equation that describes the shortest path through [[Spacetime]]
* [[Gravitational Wave]]: a ripple in the curvature of [[Spacetime]] that propagates through the universe
* [[Black Hole]]: a region of [[Spacetime]] where the [[Gravitational Pull]] is so strong that not even [[Light]] can escape

General Relativity has been extensively tested and confirmed through numerous [[Experiment]]s and [[Observation]]s, including the [[Bending of Light]] around massive objects and the [[Gravitational Redshift]] of light emitted from white dwarfs. It remains a cornerstone of modern [[Astrophysics]] and [[Cosmology]], and continues to be an active area of research and development.
