# Modular Arithmetic
Modular arithmetic is a system of arithmetic for [[Integers]] that "wraps around" after reaching a certain value, called the [[Modulus]]. It is a fundamental concept in [[Number Theory]] and has numerous applications in [[Cryptography]], [[Computer Science]], and other fields.

## Overview
Modular arithmetic is based on the concept of remainders when [[Integers]] are divided by a fixed positive integer, known as the modulus. The result of any arithmetic operation is reduced to its remainder when divided by the modulus. This creates a cyclic pattern, where the numbers "wrap around" after reaching the modulus.

## Concept Map
The key concepts in modular arithmetic are:
* [[Modulus]]: the fixed positive integer that determines the cycle length
* [[Remainder]]: the result of dividing an integer by the modulus
* [[Congruence]]: two integers are said to be congruent modulo n if they have the same remainder when divided by n
* [[Residue Class]]: a set of integers that have the same remainder when divided by the modulus
* [[Euler's Totient Function]]: a function that counts the number of positive integers less than or equal to n that are relatively prime to n
