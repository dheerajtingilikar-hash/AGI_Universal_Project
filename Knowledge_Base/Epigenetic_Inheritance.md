# Epigenetic Inheritance
Epigenetic inheritance refers to the transmission of [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] information from one generation to the next, without altering the underlying [[DNA|DNA sequence]].

## Overview
Epigenetic inheritance is a complex process that involves the regulation of [[Gene Expression|gene expression]] through various mechanisms, including [[DNA Methylation|DNA methylation]], [[Histone Modification|histone modification]], and [[Non-Coding RNA|non-coding RNA]]. These mechanisms can be influenced by environmental factors, such as [[Diet|diet]], [[Stress|stress]], and [[Environmental Toxins|environmental toxins]], and can result in changes to an organism's [[Phenotype|phenotype]] without altering its [[Genotype|genotype]].

## Concept Map
The concept of epigenetic inheritance can be broken down into several key components:
* [[Epigenetic Mark|Epigenetic marks]]: chemical modifications to [[DNA|DNA]] or [[Histone|histones]] that regulate [[Gene Expression|gene expression]]
* [[Epigenetic Regulation|Epigenetic regulation]]: the process by which epigenetic marks are established, maintained, and removed
* [[Environmental Influence|Environmental influence]]: the impact of environmental factors on epigenetic marks and regulation
* [[Transgenerational Inheritance|Transgenerational inheritance]]: the transmission of epigenetic information from one generation to the next
* [[Evolutionary Implications|Evolutionary implications]]: the potential impact of epigenetic inheritance on the evolution of [[Species|species]] and the development of [[Disease|diseases]]
