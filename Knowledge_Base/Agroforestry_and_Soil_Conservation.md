# Agroforestry and Soil Conservation

## Overview

Agroforestry is a practice that involves integrating trees into farming systems to promote ecological interactions and synergies between trees and crops. This approach aims to improve soil conservation, increase biodiversity, and enhance ecosystem services. Agroforestry systems can be designed to mitigate soil erosion, improve soil fertility, and promote soil carbon sequestration.

### Definition

Agroforestry is defined as the intentional integration of trees into farming systems to promote ecological interactions and synergies between trees and crops. [[Agroforestry]]

### Benefits

- **Soil Conservation**: Agroforestry systems can reduce soil erosion by up to 90% through the use of tree roots, leaf litter, and other vegetation. [[Soil Erosion]]
- **Soil Fertility**: Trees in agroforestry systems can fix nitrogen, improve soil structure, and increase soil organic matter. [[Soil Fertility]]
- **Biodiversity**: Agroforestry systems can promote biodiversity by providing habitat for wildlife, improving pollination, and supporting beneficial insects. [[Biodiversity]]
- **Climate Change Mitigation**: Agroforestry systems can sequester carbon in soils and trees, reducing greenhouse gas emissions. [[Climate Change]]

## Methods

- **Silvopasture**: Integrating trees into pasture systems to promote ecological interactions between trees and livestock. [[Silvopasture]]
- **Agroforestry Systems**: Designing and managing agroforestry systems to promote ecological interactions between trees and crops. [[Agroforestry Systems]]
- **Permaculture**: Designing and managing sustainable ecosystems that mimic natural ecosystems. [[Permaculture]]

## Challenges

- **Land Tenure**: Secure land tenure is essential for agroforestry adoption. [[Land Tenure]]
- **Climate Change**: Climate change can impact agroforestry systems, making them more vulnerable to drought and other extreme weather events. [[Climate Change]]
- **Market Access**: Agroforestry products may not have market access, making it difficult for farmers to sell their products. [[Market Access]]
