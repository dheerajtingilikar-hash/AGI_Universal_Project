# Artillery Systems and Ballistics

## Overview

Artillery systems and ballistics are the study of the design, development, and operation of artillery weapons and their projectiles. Artillery systems include guns, howitzers, mortars, and rocket artillery, which are used to deliver high-explosive shells, projectiles, and other munitions onto a target.

### History

The development of artillery systems dates back to ancient times, with early examples including catapults and trebuchets. Modern artillery systems have evolved significantly, with advances in materials, design, and technology.

### Types of Artillery Systems

- **Field Artillery**: Designed for mobility and rapid deployment, field artillery systems are typically mounted on wheeled or tracked vehicles.
- **Towed Artillery**: Towed artillery systems are mounted on trailers and require a separate prime mover for transportation.
- **Self-Propelled Artillery**: Self-propelled artillery systems are mounted on a vehicle and have their own propulsion system.
- **Rocket Artillery**: Rocket artillery systems use rockets to deliver projectiles, often with a higher range and accuracy than traditional artillery.

## Ballistics

Ballistics is the study of the trajectory of projectiles in flight. Key factors that affect ballistics include:

- **Range**: The maximum distance a projectile can travel.
- **Accuracy**: The precision with which a projectile hits its target.
- **Velocity**: The speed at which a projectile travels.
- **Trajectory**: The curved path a projectile follows in flight.
