# Intercontinental Ballistic Missiles (ICBM)

## Overview

An Intercontinental Ballistic Missile (ICBM) is a type of ballistic missile with a range of over 5,500 kilometers (3,400 miles). ICBMs are designed to deliver nuclear warheads to targets across continents and are typically equipped with multiple independently targetable reentry vehicles (MIRVs).

### Characteristics

- **Range**: Over 5,500 kilometers (3,400 miles)
- **Payload**: Nuclear warheads
- **Propulsion**: Liquid-fueled rocket engines or solid rocket boosters
- **Guidance**: Inertial guidance systems or terrain contour matching (TERCOM)

### History

- **Development**: ICBMs were first developed in the 1950s by the Soviet Union and the United States
- **Deployment**: ICBMs were first deployed in the late 1950s and early 1960s
- **Modernization**: ICBMs have undergone numerous modernizations, including the introduction of MIRVs and advanced guidance systems
