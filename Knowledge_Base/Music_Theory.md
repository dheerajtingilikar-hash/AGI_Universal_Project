# Music Theory
## Overview
Music theory is the study of the language and practices used in creating music. It includes the study of [[Pitch]], [[Rhythm]], [[Melody]], [[Harmony]], and other aspects of music. Music theory is used to analyze and understand the structure and composition of music, as well as to create new music.

## Concept Map
* [[Acoustics]]: the study of the physical properties of sound
* [[Composition]]: the process of creating music
* [[Ear Training]]: the ability to identify and reproduce musical elements such as pitch and rhythm
* [[Form (Music)]]: the overall structure of a piece of music
* [[Harmony]]: the combination of multiple pitches sounding simultaneously
* [[Melody]]: a succession of pitches heard in sequence
* [[Musical Notation]]: the use of symbols to represent music
* [[Pitch Class]]: a group of pitches that are equivalent in sound
* [[Rhythm]]: the pattern of duration and accentuation of sounds
* [[Scales and Modes]]: a series of pitches used to create music
* [[Timbre]]: the unique tone color or sound quality of a musical instrument or voice

## Key Concepts
* [[Chord Progression]]: a series of [[Chord]]s used in a piece of music
* [[Counterpoint]]: the interweaving of multiple independent melodies
* [[Key Signature]]: a symbol used to indicate the [[Key (Music)]] of a piece of music
* [[Modulation (Music)]]: the process of changing the [[Key (Music)]] of a piece of music
* [[Musical Form]]: the overall structure of a piece of music

## Related Fields
* [[Acoustical Engineering]]: the application of acoustics to the design of musical instruments and performance spaces
* [[Cognitive Musicology]]: the study of the cognitive processes involved in music perception and creation
* [[Ethnomusicology]]: the study of music in different cultures and societies
* [[Music Cognition]]: the study of the mental processes involved in music perception and creation
* [[Music Psychology]]: the study of the psychological aspects of music and music-making
