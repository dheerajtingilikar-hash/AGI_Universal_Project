# Monarchy Types: Absolute vs Semi-Constitutional Guidelines

## Overview

A monarchy is a form of government where a single ruler, typically a king or queen, holds supreme power and authority. Monarchies can be classified into two main types: absolute and semi-constitutional. This entry provides an overview of these two types of monarchies and their characteristics.

### Absolute Monarchy

An absolute monarchy is a system of government where the monarch holds complete and absolute power, with no limitations or checks on their authority. The monarch is the supreme ruler and has the power to make laws, appoint officials, and dispense justice as they see fit. Examples of absolute monarchies include Saudi Arabia and Brunei.

### Semi-Constitutional Monarchy

A semi-constitutional monarchy is a system of government where the monarch's power is limited by a constitution or a set of laws. The monarch's role is largely ceremonial, and they are expected to act in accordance with the constitution and the advice of their government. Examples of semi-constitutional monarchies include the United Kingdom and Sweden.
