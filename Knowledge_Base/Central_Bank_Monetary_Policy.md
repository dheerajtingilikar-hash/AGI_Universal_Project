# Central Bank Monetary Policy
Central Bank Monetary Policy refers to the actions taken by a [[Central Bank]] to control the [[Money Supply]] and achieve specific economic objectives.

## Overview
Central Bank Monetary Policy is a crucial tool used by central banks to regulate the economy. The primary objectives of monetary policy are to maintain [[Price Stability]], promote [[Economic Growth]], and ensure [[Financial Stability]]. Central banks use various instruments, such as [[Interest Rates]] and [[Open Market Operations]], to influence the money supply and credit conditions in the economy.

## Concept Map
The concept map of Central Bank Monetary Policy includes the following key components:
* [[Monetary Policy Tools]]: [[Interest Rates]], [[Reserve Requirements]], and [[Open Market Operations]]
* [[Monetary Policy Objectives]]: [[Price Stability]], [[Economic Growth]], and [[Financial Stability]]
* [[Transmission Mechanism]]: The process by which monetary policy decisions affect the economy, including the [[Money Supply]], [[Credit Conditions]], and [[Aggregate Demand]]
* [[Monetary Policy Frameworks]]: [[Inflation Targeting]], [[Monetary Policy Rules]], and [[Macroprudential Policy]]

## Key Terms
* [[Central Bank Independence]]: The ability of a central bank to make decisions without interference from the government
* [[Monetary Policy Committee]]: A committee responsible for setting monetary policy decisions
* [[Forward Guidance]]: A communication tool used by central banks to influence market expectations about future monetary policy decisions
