# Amphibious Assault Vehicles

## Overview

Amphibious assault vehicles (AAVs) are military vehicles designed to transport troops, equipment, and supplies from ships to land in a variety of environments, including beaches, rivers, and other waterways. These vehicles are typically used by amphibious assault ships and other naval vessels to support amphibious landings.

### Characteristics

- **Mobility**: AAVs are designed to operate in both water and on land, with the ability to traverse rough terrain and navigate shallow water.
- **Capacity**: AAVs can carry a variety of payloads, including troops, equipment, and supplies, with capacities ranging from 10 to 50 tons or more.
- **Speed**: AAVs can operate at speeds of up to 20-30 km/h (12-19 mph) on land and up to 10-15 km/h (6-9 mph) in water.
- **Armament**: AAVs may be equipped with various armaments, including machine guns, cannons, and missile launchers.

## Types of Amphibious Assault Vehicles

- **Landing Craft**: Small, flat-bottomed boats used to transport troops and equipment from ships to land.
- **AAV-7A1**: A US Marine Corps AAV designed for amphibious assault operations.
- **AAV-7A3**: An upgraded version of the AAV-7A1, with improved mobility and armament.
- **BTR-60PB**: A Soviet-era AAV used by various countries, including Russia and Ukraine.
