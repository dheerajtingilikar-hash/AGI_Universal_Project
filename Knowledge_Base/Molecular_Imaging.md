# Molecular Imaging
Molecular imaging is a subfield of [[Biomedical Imaging]] that focuses on visualizing and quantifying [[Biomolecules]] and their interactions at the molecular and cellular level.

## Overview
Molecular imaging involves the use of [[Imaging Agents]] or [[Probes]] to detect and visualize specific molecular processes or targets within living organisms. This field combines principles from [[Molecular Biology]], [[Biochemistry]], and [[Imaging Physics]] to provide detailed information about the molecular mechanisms underlying various diseases and conditions. Molecular imaging has applications in [[Oncology]], [[Neurology]], and [[Cardiology]], among other fields.

## Concept Map
The concept map for molecular imaging includes the following key components:
* [[Molecular Probes]]: [[Fluorescent Probes]], [[Radioactive Probes]], and [[Magnetic Resonance Probes]] used to detect and visualize molecular targets
* [[Imaging Modalities]]: [[Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)]], [[Positron Emission Tomography (PET)]], [[Single Photon Emission Computed Tomography (SPECT)]], and [[Optical Imaging]]
* [[Image Analysis]]: [[Quantification]] and [[Visualization]] of molecular imaging data to extract biologically relevant information
* [[Applications]]: [[Cancer Research]], [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]], and [[Cardiovascular Diseases]]

## Techniques
Molecular imaging techniques include:
* [[Fluorescence Microscopy]]
* [[Bioluminescence Imaging]]
* [[Photoacoustic Imaging]]
* [[Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy (MRS)]]
