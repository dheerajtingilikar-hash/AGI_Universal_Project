# Pharmacokinetics and Drug Interactions

## Overview

Pharmacokinetics and drug interactions are crucial aspects of pharmacology that govern the behavior of drugs within the body. Pharmacokinetics is the study of how a drug is absorbed, distributed, metabolized, and excreted (ADME) by the body, while drug interactions refer to the effects of one drug on the pharmacokinetics or pharmacodynamics of another drug.

### Definition

- **Pharmacokinetics**: The study of the time course of drug absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) in the body.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: The study of the biochemical and physiological effects of drugs on the body.

### Key Concepts

- **Absorption**: The process by which a drug is taken into the bloodstream.
- **Distribution**: The movement of a drug from the bloodstream to various tissues and organs.
- **Metabolism**: The chemical transformation of a drug into its active or inactive metabolites.
- **Excretion**: The removal of a drug or its metabolites from the body.

### Pharmacokinetic Parameters

- **Clearance**: The volume of plasma from which the drug is completely removed per unit time.
- **Half-life**: The time required for the plasma concentration of a drug to decrease by half.
- **Bioavailability**: The fraction of a drug that reaches the systemic circulation.

## Drug Interactions

### Mechanisms of Drug Interactions

- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: Changes in the absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion of one drug by another.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions**: Changes in the effect of one drug by another.

### Examples of Drug Interactions

- **Metabolic Interactions**: Inhibition or induction of cytochrome P450 enzymes by one drug, affecting the metabolism of another.
- **Mechanism-Based Interactions**: Interactions that alter the mechanism of action of one drug by another.
