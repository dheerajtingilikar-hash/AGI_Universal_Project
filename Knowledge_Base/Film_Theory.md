# Film Theory
Film theory refers to the study of the principles and concepts that underlie the creation and analysis of [[Film]]. It encompasses a wide range of disciplines, including [[Cinematography]], [[Screenwriting]], and [[Film Criticism]].

## Overview
Film theory involves the examination of the technical, aesthetic, and cultural aspects of film. It draws on concepts from [[Psychology]], [[Sociology]], and [[Philosophy]] to understand the ways in which films convey meaning and engage audiences. Key areas of study in film theory include [[Narrative Structure]], [[Visual Style]], and [[Sound Design]].

## Concept Map
The following concepts are central to film theory:
* [[Auteur Theory]]: the idea that a film reflects the personal vision of its director
* [[Genre Theory]]: the study of film genres, such as [[Western (film genre)]] and [[Science Fiction]]
* [[Feminist Film Theory]]: the analysis of film from a feminist perspective, focusing on issues such as [[Representation (arts)]] and [[Gender]]
* [[Cinematic Realism]]: the attempt to create a realistic representation of the world on film
* [[Postmodern Film]]: the use of non-linear narrative and self-reflexive techniques to challenge traditional notions of storytelling
