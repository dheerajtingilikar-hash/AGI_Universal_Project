# Senolytic Therapies

## Overview

Senolytic therapies are a class of treatments aimed at eliminating senescent cells, which are cells that have reached the end of their life cycle and are no longer functional but continue to proliferate and secrete pro-inflammatory factors. This concept was first introduced by Dr. Judith Campisi and Dr. David Sinclair in the early 2010s.

### Definition

Senolytic therapies involve the use of small molecules or other interventions to selectively target and eliminate senescent cells, thereby reducing the negative impact they have on tissue function and overall health.

### Mechanism of Action

Senolytic therapies work by targeting specific pathways that are involved in the survival and proliferation of senescent cells. These pathways include:

* [[p16INK4a]]: a tumor suppressor protein that is upregulated in senescent cells
* [[p21]]: a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor that is involved in cell cycle arrest
* [[BCL-2 family]]: a group of proteins that regulate apoptosis (programmed cell death)

### Types of Senolytic Therapies

There are several types of senolytic therapies, including:

* Small molecule senolytics: these are small molecules that target specific pathways involved in senescent cell survival and proliferation
* Gene therapy: this involves the use of genes to selectively target and eliminate senescent cells
* Immunotherapy: this involves the use of the immune system to target and eliminate senescent cells
