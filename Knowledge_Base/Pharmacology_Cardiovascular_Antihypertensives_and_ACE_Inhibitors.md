# Pharmacology: Cardiovascular Antihypertensives and ACE Inhibitors

## Overview

Cardiovascular antihypertensives and ACE inhibitors are a class of medications used to treat hypertension (high blood pressure) and associated cardiovascular diseases. These medications work by targeting the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), which regulates blood pressure and fluid balance in the body.

### Mechanism of Action

* **ACE Inhibitors**: Block the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II, a potent vasoconstrictor, thereby reducing blood pressure.
* **Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs)**: Block the action of angiotensin II on its receptor, reducing blood pressure.
* **Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs)**: Block the influx of calcium ions into vascular smooth muscle cells, reducing blood pressure.
* **Diuretics**: Increase urine production, reducing fluid volume and blood pressure.
* **Beta Blockers**: Reduce sympathetic nervous system activity, reducing heart rate and blood pressure.

### Pharmacokinetics

* **Absorption**: ACE inhibitors and ARBs are absorbed orally, with peak plasma concentrations occurring within 1-2 hours.
* **Distribution**: Widely distributed throughout the body, with high concentrations in the kidneys and liver.
* **Metabolism**: Primarily metabolized by the liver, with some metabolites excreted in the urine.
* **Elimination**: Eliminated primarily through the kidneys, with a half-life ranging from 10-100 hours.
