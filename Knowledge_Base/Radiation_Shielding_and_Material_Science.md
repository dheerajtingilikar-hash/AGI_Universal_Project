# Radiation Shielding and Material Science

## Overview

Radiation shielding is the use of materials to absorb or block ionizing radiation, protecting living organisms and electronic equipment from its harmful effects. The primary goal of radiation shielding is to reduce the exposure of individuals and materials to ionizing radiation, which can cause damage to DNA, cells, and tissues.

### Types of Radiation Shielding

- **Passive Shielding**: The use of materials to absorb or block radiation, such as lead, concrete, or water.
- **Active Shielding**: The use of electromagnetic fields to deflect or absorb radiation, such as magnetic fields or plasma shields.

### Radiation Shielding Materials

- **Lead**: A dense, heavy metal with high atomic number, effective at absorbing gamma radiation.
- **Concrete**: A composite material made from cement, aggregate, and water, effective at absorbing gamma radiation.
- **Water**: A low-cost, effective material for absorbing neutron radiation.
- **Hydrogen-rich materials**: Materials with high hydrogen content, such as polyethylene or liquid hydrogen, effective at absorbing neutron radiation.
- **Boron carbide**: A ceramic material with high neutron absorption cross-section, effective at absorbing neutron radiation.
