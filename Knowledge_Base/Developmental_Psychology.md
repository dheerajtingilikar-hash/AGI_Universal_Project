# Developmental Psychology
Developmental psychology is the [[Scientific Study]] of human development across the [[Lifespan]], from [[Prenatal Development]] to [[Old Age]]. It examines the [[Cognitive]], [[Social]], and [[Emotional Development]] of individuals, and the factors that influence these processes.

## Overview
Developmental psychology is an [[Interdisciplinary Field]] that draws on [[Psychology]], [[Biology]], [[Sociology]], and [[Anthropology]] to understand human development. It involves the study of [[Developmental Stages]], including [[Infancy]], [[Childhood]], [[Adolescence]], [[Adulthood]], and [[Old Age]]. Developmental psychologists use a range of [[Research Methods]], including [[Longitudinal Studies]] and [[Cross-Sectional Studies]], to investigate developmental processes and outcomes.

## Concept Map
The concept map of developmental psychology includes the following key topics:
* [[Cognitive Development]]: the study of [[Perception]], [[Attention]], [[Memory]], and [[Language Development]]
* [[Social Development]]: the study of [[Social Learning]], [[Attachment Theory]], and [[Social Identity]]
* [[Emotional Development]]: the study of [[Emotional Intelligence]], [[Emotional Regulation]], and [[Mental Health]]
* [[Developmental Theories]]: including [[Piaget's Theory]] of cognitive development, [[Erikson's Theory]] of psychosocial development, and [[Vygotsky's Theory]] of sociocultural development
* [[Developmental Disorders]]: including [[Autism Spectrum Disorder]], [[Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder]], and [[Learning Disabilities]]
