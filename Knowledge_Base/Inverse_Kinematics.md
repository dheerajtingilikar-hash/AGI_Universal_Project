# Inverse Kinematics
## Overview
Inverse Kinematics is a fundamental concept in [[Robotics]], [[Computer Animation]], and [[Computer Vision]]. It refers to the process of determining the [[Joint]] angles of a [[Robot Arm]] or a [[Kinematic Chain]] that will result in a specific [[End Effector]] pose. In other words, given a desired position and [[Orientation]] of the end effector, the inverse kinematics problem seeks to find the joint angles that will achieve this pose.

## Concept Map
The concept of Inverse Kinematics is closely related to [[Forward Kinematics]], which involves calculating the end effector pose given the joint angles. The two concepts are inverses of each other, and together they form the foundation of [[Kinematics]]. Inverse Kinematics is also related to [[Motion Planning]], which involves finding a sequence of joint angles that will move the end effector from an initial pose to a goal pose while avoiding [[Obstacles]].

### Key Concepts
* [[Kinematic Chain]]: a sequence of [[Joint]]s connected by [[Link]]s
* [[End Effector]]: the last link in a kinematic chain, typically a [[Gripper]] or a [[Tool]]
* [[Joint Angle]]: the angle of rotation of a joint
* [[Pose]]: the position and [[Orientation]] of an object in space

### Applications
* [[Robotics]]: Inverse Kinematics is used to control [[Robot Arm]]s and [[Humanoid Robot]]s
* [[Computer Animation]]: Inverse Kinematics is used to create realistic character movements
* [[Computer Vision]]: Inverse Kinematics is used to track the movement of objects in a scene
