# Behavioral Economics
Behavioral economics is a subfield of [[Economics]] that studies how [[Psychology|psychological]], [[Social Norms|social]], and [[Emotion|emotional]] factors influence [[Economic Decision Making|economic decisions]].

## Overview
Behavioral economics combines insights from [[Psychology]] and [[Economics]] to understand how people make decisions about money, [[Risk Management|risk]], and [[Uncertainty|uncertainty]]. It challenges the traditional [[Rational Choice Theory|rational choice theory]] by incorporating [[Cognitive Bias|cognitive biases]] and [[Heuristics|heuristics]] into the analysis of [[Decision Making|decision making]].

## Concept Map
The key concepts in behavioral economics include:
* [[Loss Aversion|Loss aversion]]: the tendency to prefer avoiding losses over acquiring gains
* [[Framing Effect|Framing effect]]: the influence of the way information is presented on decision making
* [[Anchoring Bias|Anchoring bias]]: the reliance on the first piece of information encountered when making a decision
* [[Mental Accounting|Mental accounting]]: the tendency to treat different types of money (e.g., [[Income|income]] vs. [[Wealth|wealth]]) differently
* [[Prospect Theory|Prospect theory]]: a descriptive model of decision making under [[Uncertainty|uncertainty]]
* [[Nudge Theory|Nudge theory]]: the use of subtle influences to alter people's behavior in predictable ways
