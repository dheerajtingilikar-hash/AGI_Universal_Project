# Pharmacokinetics
## Overview
Pharmacokinetics is the study of how a [[Drug]] is absorbed, distributed, metabolized, and excreted by the body. It involves the analysis of the [[Pharmacokinetic Parameters]] that govern the [[Pharmacological]] effects of a drug, including its [[Bioavailability]], [[Volume of Distribution]], [[Clearance]], and [[Half-Life]].

## Concept Map
The concept of pharmacokinetics can be broken down into several key components:
* [[Absorption (Pharmacokinetics)]]: the process by which a drug is taken into the body
* [[Distribution (Pharmacokinetics)]]: the process by which a drug is transported to its site of action
* [[Metabolism (Pharmacokinetics)]]: the process by which a drug is broken down into its [[Metabolite]]s
* [[Excretion (Pharmacokinetics)]]: the process by which a drug is removed from the body
* [[Toxicokinetics]]: the study of the relationship between the dose of a drug and its [[Toxicity]]

## Key Parameters
The following pharmacokinetic parameters are used to describe the behavior of a drug in the body:
* [[Area Under the Curve]] (AUC)
* [[Maximum Concentration]] (Cmax)
* [[Time to Maximum Concentration]] (Tmax)
* [[Half-Life]] (t1/2)
* [[Clearance (Pharmacokinetics)]] (CL)
* [[Volume of Distribution]] (Vd)
