# Macroeconomics
Macroeconomics is the branch of [[Economics]] that studies the behavior and performance of an economy as a whole. It focuses on the aggregate variables such as [[Gross Domestic Product (GDP)]], [[Inflation]], and [[Unemployment]].

## Overview
Macroeconomics examines the factors that influence the overall level of economic activity, including the [[Money Supply]], [[Fiscal Policy]], and [[Monetary Policy]]. It also analyzes the relationships between different economic variables, such as the [[Phillips Curve]], which describes the trade-off between inflation and unemployment.

## Concept Map
The key concepts in macroeconomics can be organized into the following categories:
* **National Income Accounting**: [[GDP]], [[Gross National Product (GNP)]], [[National Income (NI)]]
* **Inflation and Deflation**: [[Inflation Rate]], [[Deflation]], [[Disinflation]]
* **Unemployment**: [[Unemployment Rate]], [[Labor Force]], [[Participation Rate]]
* **Economic Growth**: [[Economic Growth Rate]], [[Productivity]], [[Investment]]
* **International Trade**: [[Balance of Payments]], [[Trade Balance]], [[Exchange Rate]]

Macroeconomics is closely related to other fields, including [[Microeconomics]], [[International Economics]], and [[Econometrics]]. It uses various tools and techniques, such as [[Macroeconomic Models]], [[Time Series Analysis]], and [[Regression Analysis]], to analyze and forecast economic trends.
