# Brain-Computer Interface Ethics

## Overview

Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) ethics is a multidisciplinary field that explores the moral and societal implications of BCI technology. As BCI systems become increasingly sophisticated, they raise concerns about data privacy, informed consent, and the potential for exploitation.

### Key Concepts

- **[[Data Privacy]]**: The protection of personal information and brain activity data collected through BCI systems.
- **[[Informed Consent]]**: The process of obtaining voluntary agreement from individuals to participate in BCI research or use BCI technology.
- **[[Exploitation]]**: The potential for BCI technology to be used for coercive or manipulative purposes.

### Ethical Considerations

- **[[Autonomy]]**: Ensuring that individuals have control over their brain activity data and BCI systems.
- **[[Beneficence]]**: Promoting the well-being and benefit of individuals and society through BCI technology.
- **[[Non-maleficence]]**: Avoiding harm to individuals and society through BCI technology.
- **[[Justice]]**: Ensuring that BCI technology is accessible and beneficial to all individuals, regardless of socioeconomic status or ability.

### Related Topics

- **[[Neuroethics]]**: The study of the ethical implications of neuroscience and neurotechnology.
- **[[Neuroprosthetics]]**: The use of BCI technology to restore or enhance cognitive function in individuals with neurological disorders.
- **[[Neural Interfaces]]**: The development of BCI systems that directly interface with the brain.
