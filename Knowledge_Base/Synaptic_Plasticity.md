# Synaptic Plasticity
## Overview
Synaptic plasticity refers to the ability of [[Synapse|synapses]] to change and adapt in response to experience, learning, and environmental factors. This concept is fundamental to understanding how the [[Brain|brain]] processes and stores information. Synaptic plasticity is a key mechanism underlying [[Learning|learning]] and [[Memory|memory]] formation, and is essential for the development and refinement of [[Neural Network|neural networks]].

## Concept Map
Synaptic plasticity can be broadly categorized into several types, including:
* [[Long-Term Potentiation|Long-term potentiation (LTP)]]: a persistent strengthening of synaptic connections
* [[Long-Term Depression|Long-term depression (LTD)]]: a persistent weakening of synaptic connections
* [[Short-Term Synaptic Plasticity|Short-term synaptic plasticity]]: transient changes in synaptic efficacy
These forms of synaptic plasticity are thought to be mediated by various cellular and molecular mechanisms, including changes in [[Neurotransmitter|neurotransmitter]] release, [[Receptor|receptor]] density, and [[Signaling Pathway|signaling pathways]].

## Key Mechanisms
Synaptic plasticity is influenced by a range of factors, including:
* [[Neurotrophic Factor|Neurotrophic factors]]: proteins that support the growth and survival of [[Neuron|neurons]]
* [[Ion Channel|Ion channels]]: proteins that regulate the flow of ions across cell membranes
* [[Gene Expression|Gene expression]]: the process by which cells regulate the production of [[Protein|proteins]]

## Related Concepts
* [[Neural Development|Neural development]]: the process by which the brain develops and matures
* [[Neuroregeneration|Neuroregeneration]]: the process by which the brain repairs and regenerates damaged tissue
* [[Neurodegenerative Disease|Neurodegenerative disease]]: conditions characterized by the progressive loss of [[Neuron|neurons]] and [[Synapse|synapses]]
