# Existentialism
Existentialism is a philosophical movement that emphasizes individual freedom and choice, and the inherent meaninglessness of life.

## Overview
Existentialism is a philosophical theory that posits that individuals must create their own meaning in life, as there is no inherent or objective meaning. This philosophy is centered around the concept of [[Existence]], and the idea that individuals must take responsibility for their own lives and choices. Existentialism is often associated with [[Philosophy]], [[Phenomenology]], and [[Humanism]].

## Concept Map
The concept of existentialism can be broken down into several key components, including:
* [[Freedom]]: The idea that individuals have the freedom to make choices and create their own meaning in life.
* [[Responsibility]]: The idea that individuals must take responsibility for their own lives and choices.
* [[Authenticity]]: The idea that individuals must be true to themselves and live authentically.
* [[Absurdism]]: The idea that the human desire for meaning and purpose in life is inherently at odds with the fact that the universe is indifferent to human existence.
* [[Subjectivism]]: The idea that reality is subjective and can only be understood through individual [[Perception]].

Key figures associated with existentialism include [[Jean-Paul Sartre]], [[Martin Heidegger]], and [[Søren Kierkegaard]]. Existentialism has had a significant influence on various fields, including [[Psychology]], [[Literature]], and [[Art]].
