# Anatomy
Anatomy is the scientific study of the structure of [[Organism]]s, including their systems, organs, and tissues.

## Overview
Anatomy is a fundamental discipline in the field of [[Biology]] and is closely related to [[Physiology]], which is the study of the functions of living organisms. The study of anatomy involves the examination of the [[Cell]]s, [[Tissue]]s, and organs that make up an organism, as well as their relationships and interactions. Anatomy is essential for understanding how organisms function, develop, and respond to their environment.

## Concept Map
The study of anatomy can be divided into several subfields, including:
* [[Gross Anatomy]], which is the study of the large-scale structure of organisms
* [[Histology]], which is the study of the microscopic structure of tissues
* [[Embryology]], which is the study of the development of organisms from [[Fertilization]] to birth
* [[Comparative Anatomy]], which is the study of the similarities and differences in the anatomy of different organisms
* [[Neuroanatomy]], which is the study of the structure and organization of the [[Nervous System]]

Anatomy is also closely related to other fields, including [[Medicine]], [[Surgery]], and [[Evolutionary Biology]]. The study of anatomy has many practical applications, including the diagnosis and treatment of diseases, the development of new medical technologies, and the understanding of the evolution of organisms over time. Key concepts in anatomy include [[Homeostasis]], [[Morphology]], and [[Phylogeny]].
