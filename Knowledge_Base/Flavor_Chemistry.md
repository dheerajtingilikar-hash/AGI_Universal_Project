# Flavor Chemistry
Flavor chemistry is the study of the chemical compounds that create the [[Flavor]] and [[Aroma]] of foods and beverages.

## Overview
Flavor chemistry involves the analysis of the chemical composition of foods and the identification of the specific compounds that contribute to their flavor and aroma. This field of study combines [[Chemistry]], [[Biochemistry]], and [[Food Science]] to understand the complex interactions between [[Molecules]] and the [[Sensory System]]. Key concepts in flavor chemistry include [[Volatiles]], [[Flavonoids]], and [[Terpenes]].

## Concept Map
* [[Flavor]]: the sensation of taste and aroma
	+ [[Taste]]: the sensation of sweetness, sourness, bitterness, and umami
	+ [[Aroma]]: the sensation of smell
* [[Volatiles]]: chemical compounds that evaporate easily and contribute to aroma
	+ [[Terpenes]]: a class of volatile compounds found in plants
	+ [[Aldehydes]]: a class of volatile compounds found in fruits and flowers
* [[Flavonoids]]: a class of plant compounds that contribute to flavor and color
	+ [[Anthocyanins]]: a subclass of flavonoids responsible for red and purple colors
	+ [[Quercetin]]: a flavonoid with potential health benefits
* [[Food Science]]: the study of the physical, chemical, and biological properties of food
	+ [[Food Chemistry]]: the study of the chemical composition of food
	+ [[Food Technology]]: the application of scientific principles to food production and processing

## Key Compounds
* [[Glutamates]]: amino acids that contribute to umami flavor
* [[Sugars]]: carbohydrates that contribute to sweetness
* [[Acids]]: compounds that contribute to sourness
* [[Bitter Compounds]]: compounds that contribute to bitterness

## Applications
* [[Food Industry]]: the application of flavor chemistry to food production and processing
* [[Perfumery]]: the application of flavor chemistry to fragrance production
* [[Pharmaceuticals]]: the application of flavor chemistry to drug development and production
