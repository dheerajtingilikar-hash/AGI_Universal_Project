# Deontology vs Utilitarianism
Deontology and Utilitarianism are two major [[Ethics|ethical]] theories that have been debated by [[Philosophy|philosophers]] and [[Ethicist|ethicists]] for centuries. 

## Overview
Deontology is an [[Ethics|ethical]] theory that emphasizes the importance of [[Moral Rule|moral rules]] and [[Duty|duties]]. It is based on the idea that certain actions are inherently right or wrong, regardless of their consequences. In contrast, Utilitarianism is a [[Consequentialism|consequentialist]] theory that argues that the right action is the one that maximizes overall [[Happiness|happiness]] or [[Well-being|well-being]]. The key difference between the two theories lies in their approach to [[Moral Decision-making|moral decision-making]].

## Concept Map
* [[Deontology]]
	+ Emphasizes [[Moral Rule|moral rules]] and [[Duty|duties]]
	+ Based on the idea that certain actions are inherently right or wrong
	+ Associated with [[Immanuel Kant|Immanuel Kant]] and his concept of the [[Categorical Imperative]]
* [[Utilitarianism]]
	+ Argues that the right action is the one that maximizes overall [[Happiness|happiness]] or [[Well-being|well-being]]
	+ Based on the idea that the consequences of an action determine its moral value
	+ Associated with [[Jeremy Bentham|Jeremy Bentham]] and [[John Stuart Mill|John Stuart Mill]]
* Key differences
	+ [[Deontology]] focuses on [[Moral Rule|moral rules]] and [[Duty|duties]], while [[Utilitarianism]] focuses on [[Consequences|consequences]]
	+ [[Deontology]] is a non-[[Consequentialism|consequentialist]] theory, while [[Utilitarianism]] is a [[Consequentialism|consequentialist]] theory
