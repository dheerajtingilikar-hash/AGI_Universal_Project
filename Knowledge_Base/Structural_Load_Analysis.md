# Structural Load Analysis
## Overview
Structural Load Analysis is a critical process in [[Civil Engineering]] and [[Architecture]] that involves evaluating the [[Stress]] and [[Strain]] on a structure due to various types of [[Load]]s. The primary objective of this analysis is to ensure that the structure can withstand external forces such as [[Wind Load]], [[Seismic Load]], and [[Dead Load]] without compromising its [[Integrity]].

## Concept Map
The concept of Structural Load Analysis can be broken down into several key components:
* [[Load Calculation]]: determining the magnitude and distribution of loads on the structure
* [[Stress Analysis]]: evaluating the resulting [[Stress]] and [[Strain]] on the structure
* [[Material Properties]]: considering the properties of the materials used in the structure, such as [[Modulus of Elasticity]] and [[Yield Strength]]
* [[Structural System]]: understanding the behavior of the structure as a whole, including its [[Geometry]] and [[Boundary Conditions]]
* [[Design Criteria]]: ensuring that the structure meets the required [[Safety Factor]] and [[Serviceability]] standards

## Types of Loads
Structural Load Analysis involves considering various types of loads, including:
* [[Dead Load]]: the weight of the structure itself
* [[Live Load]]: the weight of occupants, furniture, and other movable objects
* [[Wind Load]]: the force exerted by wind on the structure
* [[Seismic Load]]: the force exerted by earthquakes on the structure
* [[Snow Load]]: the weight of snow on the structure

## Analysis Techniques
Several analysis techniques are used in Structural Load Analysis, including:
* [[Finite Element Method]]: a numerical method for analyzing complex structures
* [[Classical Methods]]: traditional methods for analyzing simple structures, such as [[Beam Theory]] and [[Column Theory]]
* [[Dynamic Analysis]]: analyzing the response of the structure to time-dependent loads, such as earthquakes and wind.
