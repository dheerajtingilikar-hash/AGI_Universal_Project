# Keynesian vs Austrian Economics
## Overview
Keynesian economics and Austrian economics are two distinct schools of thought in the field of [[Economics]]. Keynesian economics, developed by [[John Maynard Keynes]], emphasizes the role of government intervention in stabilizing the economy, particularly during times of economic downturn. In contrast, Austrian economics, developed by [[Carl Menger]] and later refined by [[Friedrich Hayek]] and [[Ludwig von Mises]], advocates for a laissez-faire approach, arguing that the economy is best left to self-regulate.

## Concept Map
* [[Macroeconomics]]: Keynesian economics focuses on aggregate demand and the role of government in stimulating economic growth, while Austrian economics emphasizes the importance of individual decision-making and the limitations of government intervention.
* [[Fiscal Policy]]: Keynesian economists support the use of government spending and taxation to stabilize the economy, whereas Austrian economists argue that such interventions can lead to [[Inflation]] and [[Market Distortions]].
* [[Monetary Policy]]: Austrian economists are critical of central banking and the manipulation of interest rates, which they believe can create [[Economic Bubbles]] and exacerbate economic instability. In contrast, Keynesian economists see a role for monetary policy in stabilizing the economy.
* [[Free Market]]: Austrian economics is closely associated with the concept of the free market, which is seen as a self-regulating system that allocates resources efficiently. Keynesian economists, on the other hand, argue that the free market can lead to [[Market Failure]] and that government intervention is necessary to correct such failures.
* [[Economic Crisis]]: The two schools of thought have different explanations for the causes of economic crises. Austrian economists attribute crises to government intervention and the distortion of market signals, while Keynesian economists blame a lack of aggregate demand and insufficient government intervention.
