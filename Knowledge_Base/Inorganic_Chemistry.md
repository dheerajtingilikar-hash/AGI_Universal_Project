# Inorganic Chemistry
Inorganic chemistry is the branch of [[Chemistry]] that deals with the study of [[Inorganic Compound|inorganic compounds]], which are typically derived from [[Mineral]]s and do not contain [[Carbon]]-[[Hydrogen]] bonds.

## Overview
Inorganic chemistry involves the study of the properties, composition, and reactions of inorganic compounds, which can be found in a wide range of materials, including [[Mineral]]s, [[Metal]]s, and [[Salt]]s. Inorganic chemists use various techniques, such as [[Spectroscopy]] and [[Chromatography]], to analyze and characterize inorganic compounds.

## Concept Map
Inorganic chemistry can be divided into several subfields, including:
* [[Coordination Chemistry]], which deals with the study of [[Coordination Compound|coordination compounds]] and their properties
* [[Organometallic Chemistry]], which involves the study of [[Organometallic Compound|organometallic compounds]] and their reactions
* [[Bioinorganic Chemistry]], which explores the role of [[Metal]]s in [[Biology]] and [[Medicine]]
* [[Materials Science]], which involves the development of new materials with specific properties, such as [[Nanomaterial]]s and [[Ceramic]]s

Inorganic chemistry is closely related to other fields, including [[Physical Chemistry]], [[Analytical Chemistry]], and [[Geology]]. It has numerous applications in various industries, including [[Pharmaceutical]]s, [[Energy]], and [[Environmental Science]].
