# Sanskrit: The Vedic Origins of South Asian Languages

## Overview

Sanskrit is an ancient Indo-European language that originated in the Indian subcontinent, specifically in the Vedic period (1500 BCE - 500 BCE). It is the oldest known member of the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family. Sanskrit is the language of the Vedas, the oldest Hindu scriptures, and is considered a sacred language in Hinduism.

### Etymology

The word "Sanskrit" is derived from the Sanskrit word "saṃskṛta," which means "refined" or "cultivated." This term was used to distinguish Sanskrit from the vernacular languages spoken in ancient India.

### History

Sanskrit emerged as a distinct language during the Vedic period, when the Indo-Aryans migrated to the Indian subcontinent from Central Asia. The language was heavily influenced by the Dravidian languages spoken by the indigenous population of the region.

### Language Family

Sanskrit is a member of the Indo-European language family, specifically the Indo-Aryan branch. It is closely related to other ancient Indo-European languages such as Greek, Latin, and Old Persian.
