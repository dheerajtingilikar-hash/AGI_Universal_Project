# Pathophysiology: Cytokine Storm and Systemic Inflammation

## Overview

A cytokine storm and systemic inflammation refer to a severe and excessive immune response characterized by the overproduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines, leading to tissue damage and organ dysfunction. This phenomenon is often associated with severe infections, autoimmune disorders, and cancer.

### Definition

- **Cytokine storm**: A sudden and excessive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, leading to tissue damage and organ dysfunction.
- **Systemic inflammation**: A widespread inflammatory response affecting multiple organs and tissues.

### Etiology

- **Severe infections**: Bacterial, viral, or fungal infections can trigger a cytokine storm and systemic inflammation.
- **Autoimmune disorders**: Conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, and multiple sclerosis can lead to chronic inflammation and tissue damage.
- **Cancer**: Tumor cells can induce a cytokine storm and systemic inflammation, promoting tumor growth and metastasis.

### Pathophysiology

- **Cytokine release**: Activated immune cells release pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6.
- **Inflammation**: Cytokines induce the expression of adhesion molecules, leading to the recruitment of immune cells to the site of inflammation.
- **Tissue damage**: Excessive inflammation and cytokine release cause tissue damage and organ dysfunction.
