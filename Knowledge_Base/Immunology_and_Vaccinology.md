# Immunology and Vaccinology

## Overview

Immunology and Vaccinology are interdisciplinary fields that study the immune system and its responses to pathogens, as well as the development and application of vaccines to prevent infectious diseases.

### Definition

- **Immunology**: The study of the immune system, including its cells, tissues, and organs, and their interactions with pathogens and foreign substances.
- **Vaccinology**: The study of vaccines, including their development, testing, and application to prevent infectious diseases.

### Branches of Immunology

- **Cellular Immunology**: The study of immune cells, such as T cells and B cells, and their roles in immune responses.
- **Molecular Immunology**: The study of the molecular mechanisms underlying immune responses, including the structure and function of immune molecules.
- **Clinical Immunology**: The study of the diagnosis and treatment of immune-related disorders, such as allergies and autoimmune diseases.

### Types of Vaccines

- **Inactivated Vaccines**: Vaccines that contain inactivated or killed pathogens, such as influenza vaccines.
- **Live, Attenuated Vaccines**: Vaccines that contain weakened or attenuated pathogens, such as measles vaccines.
- **Subunit Vaccines**: Vaccines that contain only specific components of pathogens, such as hepatitis B vaccines.
- **Conjugate Vaccines**: Vaccines that combine a weakened pathogen with a carrier protein, such as pneumococcal conjugate vaccines.
