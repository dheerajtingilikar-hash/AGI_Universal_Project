# Pharmacology: Analgesics - NSAIDs vs Opioid Management

## Overview

Analgesics are a class of medications used to relieve pain. Two primary categories of analgesics are Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) and opioids. This entry provides an overview of the differences between NSAIDs and opioids, including their mechanisms of action, indications, and side effects.

### Mechanism of Action

#### NSAIDs

- **Mechanism**: Inhibit the enzyme cyclooxygenase (COX), which is involved in the synthesis of prostaglandins, leading to reduced inflammation and pain.
- **Types**: Include ibuprofen, naproxen, and diclofenac.
- **Indications**: Relieve mild to moderate pain, inflammation, and fever.
- **Side Effects**: Gastrointestinal issues, renal impairment, and increased risk of cardiovascular events.

#### Opioids

- **Mechanism**: Bind to opioid receptors in the brain, spinal cord, and other areas, altering pain perception and emotional response.
- **Types**: Include morphine, oxycodone, and fentanyl.
- **Indications**: Relieve moderate to severe pain, including acute and chronic pain.
- **Side Effects**: Respiratory depression, constipation, nausea, and dependence.
