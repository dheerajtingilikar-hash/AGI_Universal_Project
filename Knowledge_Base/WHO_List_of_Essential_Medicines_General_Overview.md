# WHO List of Essential Medicines (General Overview)

## Overview

The WHO List of Essential Medicines (EML) is a list of minimum medicine requirements for a health system, published by the World Health Organization (WHO). It is designed to provide a basis for national lists of essential medicines, which should be available and accessible to all people in need.

### Purpose

The primary purpose of the EML is to ensure that people have access to the medicines they need to treat common, serious, and life-threatening conditions. The list is updated every two years to reflect changes in medical science, new technologies, and emerging health issues.

### History

The first EML was published in 1977, and it has since been updated 11 times. The list has undergone significant changes over the years, with the addition of new medicines and the removal of others.

### Process

1. **Selection of medicines**
2. **Review and evaluation**
3. **Consultation with experts**
4. **Approval by the WHO Expert Committee on the Selection and Use of Essential Medicines**

### Updates

The EML is updated every two years, with the following process:

1. **Call for nominations**
2. **Review and evaluation**
3. **Consultation with experts**
4. **Approval by the WHO Expert Committee on the Selection and Use of Essential Medicines**

### Availability

The EML is available in multiple languages and can be accessed on the WHO website.
