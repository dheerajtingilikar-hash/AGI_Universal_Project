# AGI Architecture
## Overview
[[Artificial General Intelligence]] (AGI) architecture refers to the design and structure of systems intended to achieve [[Artificial Intelligence]] (AI) that can perform any intellectual task that a human can. AGI architecture is a key area of research in the field of AI, as it aims to create systems that can learn, reason, and apply knowledge across a wide range of tasks.

## Concept Map
The AGI architecture concept map includes the following key components:
* [[Cognitive Architecture]]: the overall structure of the system, including the relationships between different components and the flow of information
* [[Knowledge Representation]]: the way in which the system stores and represents knowledge, including [[Ontology]] and [[Semantic Network]]s
* [[Reasoning]] and [[Inference]]: the mechanisms by which the system draws conclusions and makes decisions based on available knowledge
* [[Learning]] and [[Adaptation]]: the ability of the system to acquire new knowledge and adjust to changing circumstances
* [[Natural Language Processing]]: the ability of the system to understand and generate human language

## Key Technologies
AGI architecture relies on a range of key technologies, including:
* [[Machine Learning]]: a type of [[AI]] that enables systems to learn from data
* [[Deep Learning]]: a subset of machine learning that uses [[Neural Network]]s to analyze data
* [[Computer Vision]]: the ability of systems to interpret and understand visual data
* [[Robotics]]: the integration of AGI with physical systems, such as robots, to interact with the environment

## Related Fields
AGI architecture is related to a number of other fields, including:
* [[Cognitive Science]]: the study of the human mind and its processes
* [[Neuroscience]]: the study of the structure and function of the brain
* [[Philosophy of Mind]]: the study of the nature of consciousness and the mind
