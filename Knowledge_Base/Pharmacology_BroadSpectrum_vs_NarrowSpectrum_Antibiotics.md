# Pharmacology: Broad-Spectrum vs Narrow-Spectrum Antibiotics

## Overview

Broad-spectrum antibiotics and narrow-spectrum antibiotics are two categories of antimicrobial agents that differ in their spectrum of activity. Broad-spectrum antibiotics are effective against a wide range of microorganisms, including both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, as well as some fungi and viruses. Narrow-spectrum antibiotics, on the other hand, are effective against a narrower range of microorganisms, typically targeting specific types of bacteria.

### Definition

- **Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics**: [[Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics]] are antimicrobial agents that are effective against a wide range of microorganisms, including both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, as well as some fungi and viruses.
- **Narrow-Spectrum Antibiotics**: [[Narrow-Spectrum Antibiotics]] are antimicrobial agents that are effective against a narrower range of microorganisms, typically targeting specific types of bacteria.

### Mechanism of Action

- **Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics**: Broad-spectrum antibiotics often work by inhibiting cell wall synthesis, protein synthesis, or DNA replication in microorganisms. Examples include [[Cephalosporins]], [[Penicillins]], and [[Fluoroquinolones]].
- **Narrow-Spectrum Antibiotics**: Narrow-spectrum antibiotics often work by targeting specific enzymes or metabolic pathways in microorganisms. Examples include [[Macrolides]], [[Lincosamides]], and [[Aminoglycosides]].

### Clinical Use

- **Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics**: Broad-spectrum antibiotics are often used to treat severe infections, such as sepsis, or when the causative organism is unknown. Examples include [[Ciprofloxacin]] and [[Amoxicillin-Clavulanate]].
- **Narrow-Spectrum Antibiotics**: Narrow-spectrum antibiotics are often used to treat mild to moderate infections, such as respiratory tract infections or skin infections. Examples include [[Azithromycin]] and [[Clindamycin]].

### Resistance

- **Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics**: Broad-spectrum antibiotics are more likely to contribute to the development of antibiotic resistance due to their widespread use and potential for overuse.
- **Narrow-Spectrum Antibiotics**: Narrow-spectrum antibiotics are less likely to contribute to the development of antibiotic resistance due to their targeted use and lower potential for overuse.
