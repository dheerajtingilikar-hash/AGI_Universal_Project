# Cognitive Bias and Heuristics
Cognitive biases and heuristics refer to the systematic patterns of deviation from normative or rational judgment, often resulting from mental shortcuts or rules of thumb.

## Overview
Cognitive biases and heuristics are inherent in human thinking, influencing decision-making, perception, and judgment. They can lead to errors in reasoning, memory, and decision-making, often resulting in irrational or inaccurate conclusions. [[Cognitive Psychology]] studies these biases and heuristics to understand their underlying mechanisms and effects on human behavior. Key types of cognitive biases include [[Confirmation Bias]], [[Anchoring Bias]], and [[Availability Heuristic]].

## Concept Map
The concept of cognitive biases and heuristics can be mapped as follows:
- **Biases**: 
  - [[Confirmation Bias]]: tendency to favor information that confirms pre-existing beliefs
  - [[Anchoring Bias]]: reliance on initial information when making subsequent judgments
  - [[Hindsight Bias]]: tendency to believe, after an event has occurred, that it was predictable
- **Heuristics**: 
  - [[Availability Heuristic]]: judging likelihood based on how easily examples come to mind
  - [[Representativeness Heuristic]]: judging likelihood based on how closely a scenario resembles a typical case
  - [[Affect Heuristic]]: making decisions based on emotional responses
- **Theories**: 
  - [[Prospect Theory]]: describes how people make decisions under uncertainty
  - [[Dual-Process Theory]]: proposes that thinking can be divided into two modes: intuitive and reflective
- **Applications**: 
  - [[Decision Making]]: understanding cognitive biases and heuristics to improve decision-making processes
  - [[Critical Thinking]]: recognizing and mitigating the effects of cognitive biases and heuristics
  - [[Behavioral Economics]]: studying how cognitive biases and heuristics influence economic decisions
