# Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS/GPS/GLONASS)

## Overview

Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) are a network of satellites orbiting the Earth that provide location information to GPS receivers on the ground. The most well-known GNSS is the Global Positioning System (GPS), developed by the United States. Other notable GNSS include the Russian GLONASS and the European Galileo system.

### History

- GPS was first launched in 1978 and became fully operational in 1995.
- GLONASS was launched in 1982 and became fully operational in 1995.
- Galileo was launched in 2011 and became fully operational in 2020.

### Key Components

- **Satellites**: GNSS satellites orbit the Earth at an altitude of approximately 20,000 km.
- **Ground Control**: Ground control stations monitor and control the GNSS satellites.
- **GPS Receivers**: GPS receivers on the ground use signals from GNSS satellites to determine their location.

### Types of GNSS

- **GPS (Global Positioning System)**: Developed by the United States.
- **GLONASS (Global Navigation Satellite System)**: Developed by Russia.
- **Galileo**: Developed by the European Union.
- **BeiDou**: Developed by China.
- **QZSS (Quasi-Zenith Satellite System)**: Developed by Japan.
