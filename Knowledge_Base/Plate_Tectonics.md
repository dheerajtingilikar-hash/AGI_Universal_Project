# Plate Tectonics
Plate tectonics is the theoretical framework in [[Geology]] that describes the large-scale motion of the [[Lithosphere]], the outermost solid layer of the [[Earth]]. 

## Overview
Plate tectonics is a relatively new concept, developed in the 1950s and 1960s, which revolutionized the field of geology. The theory states that the lithosphere is broken into several large plates that move relative to each other, creating and shaping the Earth's surface. These plates are in constant motion, sliding over the more fluid [[Asthenosphere]] below, resulting in the creation of [[Mountain]] ranges, [[Volcano]]es, and [[Earthquake]]s.

## Concept Map
The key concepts in plate tectonics include:
- **Plate Boundaries**: The areas where two or more plates meet, which can be [[Divergent Boundary|divergent]], [[Convergent Boundary|convergent]], or [[Transform Boundary|transform]].
- **Plate Motion**: The movement of the plates, which can be described as [[Continental Drift|continental drift]].
- **Earth's Interior**: The structure of the Earth, including the [[Crust]], [[Mantle]], [[Outer Core]], and [[Inner Core]].
- **Geologic Processes**: The formation of [[Orogeny|mountain ranges]], [[Volcanic Arc|volcanic arcs]], and other geological features as a result of plate tectonics.
