# Quantum Entanglement
## Overview
Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon in which two or more [[Particle|particles]] become correlated in such a way that the state of one particle cannot be described independently of the others, even when they are separated by large distances. This means that measuring the state of one particle will instantly affect the state of the other entangled particles. Quantum entanglement is a fundamental aspect of [[Quantum Mechanics|quantum mechanics]] and has been experimentally confirmed numerous times.

## Concept Map
- **Entangled Particles**: [[Particle|Particles]] that are correlated in such a way that the state of one particle cannot be described independently of the others.
- **Quantum Superposition**: The ability of a [[Quantum System|quantum system]] to exist in multiple states simultaneously, which is a fundamental property of [[Quantum Mechanics|quantum mechanics]].
- **Wave Function**: A mathematical description of the quantum state of a system, which encodes all the information about the system.
- **Entanglement Entropy**: A measure of the amount of entanglement between two subsystems.
- **Quantum Non-Locality**: The phenomenon where entangled particles can instantaneously affect each other, regardless of the distance between them.
