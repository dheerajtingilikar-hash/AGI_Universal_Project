# Social Stratification Theory
## Overview
Social Stratification Theory refers to the [[Sociology]] of [[Social Stratification]], which is the hierarchical arrangement of individuals or groups within a [[Society]]. This theory examines how [[Social Class]], [[Wealth]], [[Power]], and [[Status]] are distributed among members of a society, resulting in [[Inequality]] and [[Social Mobility]]. Social Stratification Theory is a fundamental concept in [[Sociology]] and is closely related to other areas of study, including [[Economics]], [[Politics]], and [[Anthropology]].

## Concept Map
The key concepts in Social Stratification Theory include:
* [[Social Class]]: a group of individuals who share similar [[Socioeconomic]] characteristics, such as [[Wealth]], [[Income]], and [[Occupation]]
* [[Caste System]]: a rigid form of social stratification in which individuals are born into a particular [[Social Class]] and remain in it for their entire lives
* [[Social Mobility]]: the movement of individuals or groups from one [[Social Class]] to another, either upward or downward
* [[Meritocracy]]: a system in which individuals are rewarded based on their [[Talent]] and [[Effort]], rather than their [[Birth]] or [[Social Status]]
* [[Institutionalized Inequality]]: the ways in which [[Social Institutions]], such as [[Education]] and [[Employment]], perpetuate [[Inequality]] and limit [[Social Mobility]]

## Key Theorists
* [[Karl Marx]]: developed the concept of [[Class Conflict]] and the idea that [[Social Class]] is based on [[Economic]] relationships
* [[Max Weber]]: expanded on Marx's ideas and developed the concept of [[Status]] and [[Power]] in relation to [[Social Stratification]]
* [[Pierre Bourdieu]]: introduced the concept of [[Cultural Capital]] and its role in shaping [[Social Stratification]] and [[Inequality]]
