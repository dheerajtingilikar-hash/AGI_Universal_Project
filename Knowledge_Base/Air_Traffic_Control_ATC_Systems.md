# Air Traffic Control (ATC) Systems

## Overview

Air traffic control (ATC) systems are critical components of the aviation industry, responsible for ensuring the safe and efficient movement of aircraft through the national airspace system. ATC systems use a combination of technology, procedures, and human expertise to manage air traffic, preventing collisions and minimizing delays.

### Definition

Air traffic control is defined as the management of aircraft movements on the ground and in the air to prevent collisions, minimize delays, and maximize the capacity of the airspace.

### History

The first air traffic control system was established in the United States in 1929, with the creation of the Aeronautical Radio, Incorporated (ARINC) system. This system used radio communication to guide pilots through the airspace. Over the years, ATC systems have evolved to incorporate new technologies, such as radar and computer systems.

### Components

ATC systems consist of several key components, including:

* **Air Traffic Control Centers (ATCCs)**: These are the central facilities that manage air traffic over a specific region.
* **Approach Control**: This is the facility responsible for guiding aircraft during the approach and landing phases.
* **Tower Control**: This is the facility responsible for guiding aircraft during takeoff and landing.
* **Radar**: This is the technology used to track the movement of aircraft.
* **Communication Systems**: These are the systems used to communicate with pilots and other air traffic control facilities.
