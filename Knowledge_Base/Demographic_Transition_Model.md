# Demographic Transition Model
The Demographic Transition Model is a [[Demography|demographic]] model that describes how the population of a country changes over time through four phases.

## Overview
The Demographic Transition Model is based on the idea that as a country develops economically, its population growth rate and [[Fertility Rate|fertility rate]] will change. The model was first proposed by [[Warren Thompson|Warren S. Thompson]] in 1929 and is widely used in [[Population Studies|population studies]]. The four phases of the model are:
1. **Phase 1: High Fluctuating**: High [[Birth Rate|birth rate]] and high [[Death Rate|death rate]]
2. **Phase 2: Early Expanding**: High birth rate and declining death rate
3. **Phase 3: Late Expanding**: Declining birth rate and low death rate
4. **Phase 4: Low Fluctuating**: Low birth rate and low death rate

## Concept Map
The Demographic Transition Model can be represented as a [[Concept Map|concept map]]:
- **Phase 1**: High birth rate → High death rate → High [[Mortality Rate|mortality rate]]
- **Phase 2**: Improvements in [[Healthcare|healthcare]] → Declining death rate → Rapid [[Population Growth|population growth]]
- **Phase 3**: Improvements in [[Family Planning|family planning]] → Declining birth rate → Slowing population growth
- **Phase 4**: Low birth rate → Low death rate → Stable population size
The Demographic Transition Model is related to other demographic concepts, including [[Population Pyramid|population pyramid]] and [[Life Expectancy|life expectancy]].
