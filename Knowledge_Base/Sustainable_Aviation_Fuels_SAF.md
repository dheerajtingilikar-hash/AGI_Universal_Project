# Sustainable Aviation Fuels (SAF)

## Overview

Sustainable Aviation Fuels (SAF) are a type of fuel designed to reduce the environmental impact of air travel. They are produced from renewable biomass sources, such as waste organic materials, agricultural residues, or algae, and can be used in conventional aircraft engines with minimal modifications.

### Definition

SAF is defined by the International Air Transport Association (IATA) as a fuel that meets the following criteria:

- Produced from renewable biomass sources
- Has a lifecycle greenhouse gas (GHG) emission reduction of at least 80% compared to fossil jet fuel
- Meets the same technical specifications as fossil jet fuel

### Benefits

- Reduces GHG emissions by up to 80%
- Can be used in conventional aircraft engines with minimal modifications
- Can be produced from a variety of renewable biomass sources
- Can help to reduce dependence on fossil fuels

### Production Methods

- **Algal Oil Production**: Algae are grown in photobioreactors or open ponds and then harvested for oil extraction.
- **Waste-to-Fuel**: Organic waste is converted into bio-oil through a process of pyrolysis or gasification.
- **Agricultural Residue Utilization**: Agricultural residues, such as corn stover or wheat straw, are converted into bio-oil through a process of pyrolysis or gasification.
