# Satellite Communications (SATCOM)

## Overview

Satellite Communications (SATCOM) is a method of transmitting information through a network of artificial satellites and ground stations. This technology enables communication over long distances, bypassing terrestrial infrastructure and providing global coverage.

### Definition

- **[[Satellite]]**: An artificial object that orbits a celestial body, used for communication, navigation, and other purposes.
- **[[Communication]]**: The exchange of information between two or more entities, including humans, machines, or systems.

### History

- **[[1950s]]**: The first commercial SATCOM system was launched by the United States in the 1950s, using a network of satellites to provide telephone and telegraph services.
- **[[1960s]]**: The Soviet Union launched its first SATCOM system, known as Molniya, which provided communication services to the Soviet Union's remote regions.
- **[[1970s]]**: The first geostationary satellites were launched, enabling continuous communication between satellites and ground stations.

## Types of SATCOM Systems

- **[[Geostationary Satellite]]**: A satellite that orbits the Earth at an altitude of approximately 36,000 km, remaining stationary relative to a fixed point on the equator.
- **[[Low Earth Orbit (LEO) Satellite]]**: A satellite that orbits the Earth at an altitude of less than 2,000 km, requiring a network of satellites to provide continuous coverage.
- **[[Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) Satellite]]**: A satellite that orbits the Earth at an altitude of between 2,000 km and 36,000 km, offering a balance between LEO and geostationary satellites.

## Applications

- **[[Telecommunications]]**: SATCOM systems provide communication services, including voice, data, and video transmission.
- **[[Navigation]]**: SATCOM systems are used in navigation systems, such as GPS, to provide location and timing information.
- **[[Weather Forecasting]]**: SATCOM systems are used to transmit weather data from satellites to ground stations.
