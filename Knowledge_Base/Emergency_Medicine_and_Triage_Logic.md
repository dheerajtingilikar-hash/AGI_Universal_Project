# Emergency Medicine and Triage Logic

## Overview

Emergency medicine and triage logic are critical components of emergency medical services (EMS) that enable healthcare professionals to prioritize and manage patients in emergency situations. Triage logic is a systematic approach to categorize patients based on the severity of their condition, ensuring that those with life-threatening injuries or illnesses receive immediate attention.

## Concept Map

### Triage Categories

- **Red** (Immediate): Life-threatening conditions requiring immediate attention
- **Yellow** (Delayed): Conditions that require treatment within a short period but are not life-threatening
- **Green** (Minor): Minor injuries or illnesses that can be treated and discharged
- **Black** (Expectant): Conditions with a poor prognosis, and treatment is not indicated

### Triage Decision-Making

- **Anatomy-based triage**: Focuses on the severity of injuries to the head, neck, and torso
- **Physiological-based triage**: Focuses on vital signs, such as pulse, breathing rate, and blood pressure
- **Functional-based triage**: Focuses on the patient's ability to perform basic functions, such as walking or speaking

### Triage Tools

- **Triage algorithms**: Step-by-step decision-making guides for triage
- **Triage scoring systems**: Numerical values assigned to patients based on their condition
- **Triage checklists**: Lists of critical information to consider when triaging patients
