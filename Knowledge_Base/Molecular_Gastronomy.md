# Molecular Gastronomy
Molecular gastronomy is a subdiscipline of [[Food Science]] that applies [[Scientific Method]]s to the understanding and transformation of [[Ingredients]] and [[Cooking Techniques]].

## Overview
Molecular gastronomy is a [[Food Science]] discipline that studies the physical and chemical transformations of [[Ingredients]] during [[Cooking]]. It uses concepts from [[Chemistry]], [[Physics]], and [[Biology]] to understand the behavior of [[Molecules]] in [[Food Systems]]. This field was pioneered by [[Physicist]] [[Nicholas Kurti]] and [[Chemist]] [[Hervé This]].

## Concept Map
The key concepts in molecular gastronomy include:
* [[Emulsion]]: a mixture of two or more [[Liquids]] that don't normally mix, such as [[Oil]] and [[Water]]
* [[Gelification]]: the process of forming a [[Gel]] from a [[Liquid]] using [[Gelling Agents]] like [[Agar]] or [[Carrageenan]]
* [[Spherification]]: the process of forming [[Bitesize]] [[Spheres]] of [[Liquid]] using [[Sodium Alginate]] and [[Calcium Chloride]]
* [[Foamification]]: the process of creating [[Foams]] using [[Surfactants]] like [[Lecithin]] or [[Mono- and Diglycerides]]
* [[Crystalization]]: the process of forming [[Crystals]] from a [[Solution]] using [[Sugar]] or [[Salt]]
