# UNESCO Endangered Languages and Revitalization Efforts

## Overview

The UNESCO Endangered Languages Programme is a global initiative aimed at documenting and preserving endangered languages. The programme was established in 1993 to raise awareness about the importance of linguistic diversity and the need to protect endangered languages.

### Definition

[[Endangered language]]: A language that is at risk of falling out of use and disappearing as a native language.

### Statistics

- According to the [[UNESCO Atlas of the World's Languages in Danger]], there are approximately 2,301 languages at risk of falling out of use.
- It is estimated that one language goes extinct every 14 days.

## Revitalization Efforts

### Methods

- [[Language documentation]]: The process of recording and analyzing a language's grammar, vocabulary, and syntax.
- [[Language education]]: The teaching of a language in schools or community programs.
- [[Community engagement]]: The involvement of community members in language revitalization efforts.

### Success Stories

- The [[Wampanoag language]]: A language spoken by the Wampanoag people of Massachusetts, USA, which was declared extinct in 1993 but has since been revitalized through community efforts.
- The [[Maori language]]: A language spoken in New Zealand, which was declared an official language in 1987 and has since been promoted through language education and community programs.
