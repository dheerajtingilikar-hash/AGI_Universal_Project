# Mycelial Networks and Fungal Intelligence

## Overview

Mycelial networks and fungal intelligence refer to the complex systems of interconnected fungal hyphae that facilitate communication, cooperation, and problem-solving among individual fungal organisms. This phenomenon has been observed in various species of fungi, including mycorrhizal fungi, and has been studied extensively in the fields of mycology, ecology, and cognitive science.

### Definition

Mycelial networks are complex networks of fungal hyphae that connect individual fungal organisms, allowing for the exchange of nutrients, signals, and information. Fungal intelligence, on the other hand, refers to the collective intelligence that emerges from the interactions and behaviors of individual fungal organisms within these networks.

### Characteristics

- **Decentralized**: Mycelial networks are decentralized systems, meaning that there is no central hub or controller. Instead, decision-making is distributed among individual fungal organisms.
- **Adaptive**: Mycelial networks are highly adaptive, allowing them to respond to changing environmental conditions and optimize their behavior accordingly.
- **Self-organizing**: Mycelial networks are self-organizing systems, meaning that they can reorganize themselves in response to changes in the environment or the needs of individual fungal organisms.

## Related Concepts

- **Mycorrhizal fungi**: Fungi that form symbiotic relationships with plant roots, exchanging nutrients for carbohydrates.
- **Fungal communication**: The process by which fungi exchange signals and information through chemical or electrical means.
- **Swarm intelligence**: The collective intelligence that emerges from the interactions and behaviors of individual agents within a decentralized system.
