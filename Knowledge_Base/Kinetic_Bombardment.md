# Kinetic Bombardment

## Overview

Kinetic bombardment is a military tactic that employs high-velocity projectiles to destroy targets through sheer kinetic energy, rather than explosive force. This approach is often used in space warfare and anti-satellite operations.

### History

The concept of kinetic bombardment dates back to the 1960s, when the United States and the Soviet Union explored the idea of using high-speed projectiles to destroy enemy satellites. The first kinetic kill vehicle, the ASAT (Anti-Satellite) system, was developed in the 1970s.

### Mechanism

Kinetic bombardment involves launching a high-speed projectile, typically a hypervelocity vehicle, towards a target. The projectile's velocity is typically in excess of 10 km/s, allowing it to deliver a massive amount of kinetic energy to the target upon impact. The impact can cause significant damage or destruction, depending on the target's design and materials.

### Types

There are several types of kinetic projectiles, including:

* **Hypervelocity projectiles**: These projectiles are designed to achieve speeds in excess of 10 km/s, making them capable of delivering massive kinetic energy to the target.
* **Rods from God**: These are long, thin projectiles designed to penetrate deep into the target's structure, causing significant damage.
* **Kinetic kill vehicles**: These are specialized projectiles designed to destroy enemy satellites or other space-based targets.
