# Aerodynamics and Lift
## Overview
Aerodynamics and lift are fundamental concepts in the study of [[Fluid Dynamics]] and [[Aerodynamics]]. Lift is the upward force that opposes the weight of an object and enables it to fly or float. It is created by the difference in [[Air Pressure]] above and below a surface, such as a [[Wing]] or an [[Airfoil]]. The study of aerodynamics and lift is crucial in the design and development of [[Aircraft]], [[Wind Turbines]], and other devices that interact with [[Air]].

## Concept Map
The concept of aerodynamics and lift can be broken down into several key components:
* [[Bernoulli's Principle]]: describes the relationship between [[Air Pressure]] and [[Velocity]]
* [[Angle of Attack]]: the angle between the oncoming [[Airflow]] and the [[Wing]] or [[Airfoil]]
* [[Cambered Airfoil]]: a curved surface that deflects [[Air]] downward, creating a pressure difference and generating lift
* [[Drag]]: the force that opposes the motion of an object through [[Air]]
* [[Lift Coefficient]]: a dimensionless quantity that characterizes the lift generated by a surface
* [[Reynolds Number]]: a dimensionless quantity that characterizes the nature of [[Fluid Flow]]

## Key Equations
* [[Lift Equation]]: L = 0.5 \* ρ \* v^2 \* Cl \* A
* [[Drag Equation]]: D = 0.5 \* ρ \* v^2 \* Cd \* A
