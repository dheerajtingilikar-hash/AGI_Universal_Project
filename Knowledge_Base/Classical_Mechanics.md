# Classical Mechanics
Classical mechanics is a branch of [[Physics]] that deals with the study of the motion of [[Object]]s under the influence of [[Force]]s. It provides a framework for understanding and predicting the behavior of physical systems, from the motion of [[Particle]]s to the behavior of complex [[System]]s.

## Overview
Classical mechanics is based on [[Newton's Law]]s of motion and the concept of [[Energy]]. It describes the relationship between the motion of an object and the forces acting upon it, and provides a set of mathematical tools for analyzing and predicting the behavior of physical systems. Classical mechanics is a fundamental theory that has been widely used to describe a range of phenomena, from the motion of [[Planet]]s to the behavior of [[Machine]]s.

## Concept Map
The key concepts in classical mechanics include:
* [[Kinematics]]: the study of the motion of objects without considering the forces that cause the motion
* [[Dynamics]]: the study of the motion of objects under the influence of forces
* [[Energy]]: the ability of an object to do [[Work]]
* [[Momentum]]: the product of an object's [[Mass]] and [[Velocity]]
* [[Force]]: a push or pull that causes an object to change its motion
* [[Newton's Law]]s: a set of three laws that describe the relationship between the motion of an object and the forces acting upon it
* [[Oscillation]]s: the repetitive motion of an object around a fixed point
* [[Wave]]s: the transfer of energy through a medium, such as a [[String]] or [[Air]]
