# Game Theory (Nash Equilibrium)
## Overview
Game theory is the study of how people make decisions when the outcome depends on the actions of multiple individuals or parties. The [[Nash Equilibrium]] is a fundamental concept in game theory, named after [[John Nash]], who introduced it in the 1950s. It describes a state in which no player can improve their payoff or outcome by unilaterally changing their strategy, assuming all other players keep their strategies unchanged.

## Concept Map
The Nash Equilibrium is closely related to other key concepts in game theory, including:
* [[Pareto Optimality]]: a state in which no player can improve their payoff without making another player worse off
* [[Pareto Efficiency]]: a state in which no player can improve their payoff without making another player worse off, and no other player can improve their payoff without making another player worse off
* [[Prisoner's Dilemma]]: a classic game theory example that illustrates the conflict between individual and group rationality
* [[Evolutionary Game Theory]]: a branch of game theory that studies the evolution of strategies over time

The Nash Equilibrium has numerous applications in various fields, including:
* [[Economics]]: to study the behavior of firms and markets
* [[Politics]]: to analyze the behavior of countries and international relations
* [[Biology]]: to understand the evolution of cooperation and conflict in animal societies
* [[Computer Science]]: to design and analyze algorithms for decision-making and optimization problems

## Key Results
The Nash Equilibrium has several key properties and results, including:
* [[Existence Theorem]]: the Nash Equilibrium exists in every finite game
* [[Uniqueness Theorem]]: the Nash Equilibrium is unique in some games, but not in others
* [[Refinement Theorem]]: the Nash Equilibrium can be refined to eliminate undesirable equilibria

## Related Topics
* [[Cooperative Game Theory]]: the study of games in which players can form coalitions and cooperate to achieve common goals
* [[Non-Cooperative Game Theory]]: the study of games in which players act independently and make decisions based on their own self-interest
* [[Mechanism Design]]: the study of how to design mechanisms and institutions to achieve desired outcomes in games and other strategic situations.
