# Cognitive Psychology
Cognitive psychology is the branch of [[Psychology]] that focuses on the study of [[Cognition]], including [[Attention]], [[Perception]], [[Memory]], [[Language]], [[Problem-Solving]], and [[Decision-Making]].

## Overview
Cognitive psychology is an interdisciplinary field that draws on [[Neuroscience]], [[Computer Science]], [[Philosophy]], and [[Anthropology]] to understand the workings of the [[Human Brain]]. It aims to develop and test theories about the mental processes that underlie human behavior, including [[Sensation]], [[Perception]], and [[Action]]. Key areas of research in cognitive psychology include [[Learning]], [[Motivation]], and [[Emotion]].

## Concept Map
The following concept map illustrates the relationships between key concepts in cognitive psychology:
- [[Cognition]]: the mental process of acquiring, processing, and using [[Knowledge]]
- [[Attention]]: the ability to selectively focus on certain [[Stimuli]] or tasks
- [[Memory]]: the mental process of encoding, storing, and retrieving [[Information]]
- [[Language]]: the ability to understand, produce, and use [[Verbal]] and [[Nonverbal]] symbols
- [[Problem-Solving]]: the mental process of identifying, analyzing, and solving [[Problems]]
- [[Decision-Making]]: the mental process of evaluating options and selecting a course of [[Action]]

Related fields include [[Neuropsychology]], [[Artificial Intelligence]], and [[Human-Computer Interaction]]. Key researchers in the field of cognitive psychology include [[Ulric Neisser]], [[George Miller]], and [[Elizabeth Loftus]].
