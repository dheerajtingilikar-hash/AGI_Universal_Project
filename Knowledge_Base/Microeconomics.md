# Microeconomics
Microeconomics is a branch of [[Economics]] that studies the behavior and decision-making of individual economic units, such as [[Household]]s, [[Firm]]s, and [[Market]]s.

## Overview
Microeconomics examines how these units allocate [[Resource]]s to meet their [[Utility]] and [[Preference]]s. It analyzes the interactions among these units in specific [[Market Structure]]s, such as [[Perfect Competition]], [[Monopoly]], and [[Oligopoly]]. The field also explores the impact of [[Externalities]], [[Information Asymmetry]], and [[Government Intervention]] on economic outcomes.

## Concept Map
The key concepts in microeconomics include:
* [[Supply and Demand]]: the relationship between the quantity of a [[Good]] or [[Service]] that [[Firm]]s are willing to sell and the quantity that [[Household]]s are willing to buy
* [[Opportunity Cost]]: the value of the next best alternative that is given up when a choice is made
* [[Comparative Advantage]]: the ability of an individual or country to produce a [[Good]] or [[Service]] at a lower [[Opportunity Cost]] than others
* [[Market Failure]]: a situation in which the [[Market]] fails to allocate [[Resource]]s efficiently, often due to [[Externalities]] or [[Information Asymmetry]]
* [[Consumer Behavior]]: the study of how [[Household]]s make decisions about what [[Good]]s and [[Service]]s to buy
* [[Production Theory]]: the study of how [[Firm]]s make decisions about what [[Good]]s and [[Service]]s to produce and how to produce them.
