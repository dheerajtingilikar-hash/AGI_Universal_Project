# Post-Modernist Critique
The Post-Modernist Critique is a philosophical and cultural movement that emerged in the mid-20th century, characterized by a rejection of [[Modernism]] and its underlying assumptions about [[Objectivity]], [[Truth]], and [[Reality]].

## Overview
The Post-Modernist Critique is a broad and diverse movement that encompasses various fields, including [[Philosophy]], [[Literary Theory]], [[Cultural Studies]], and [[Sociology]]. It is marked by a skepticism towards [[Grand Narratives]] and [[Meta-Narratives]], which are seen as totalizing and oppressive. Instead, Post-Modernist Critique emphasizes the importance of [[Difference]], [[Diversity]], and [[Fragmentation]].

## Concept Map
The Post-Modernist Critique can be understood through the following key concepts:
* [[Deconstruction]]: a method of critical analysis that seeks to uncover the underlying power dynamics and biases in [[Texts]] and [[Discourses]].
* [[Hyperreality]]: a state in which [[Simulacra]] and [[Models]] replace [[Reality]] and [[Truth]].
* [[Post-Structuralism]]: a movement that rejects the idea of a fixed, underlying [[Structure]] to [[Language]] and [[Culture]].
* [[Power-Knowledge]]: a concept that highlights the relationship between [[Power]] and [[Knowledge]], and how they are intertwined and mutually reinforcing.
* [[Social Constructivism]]: a theory that argues that [[Reality]] is constructed through [[Social]] and [[Cultural]] processes, rather than being an objective fact.

The Post-Modernist Critique has been influential in shaping contemporary thought and culture, and continues to be a subject of debate and discussion in various fields, including [[Critical Theory]], [[Cultural Studies]], and [[Philosophy]].
