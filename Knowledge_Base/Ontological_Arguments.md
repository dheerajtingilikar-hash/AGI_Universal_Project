# Ontological Arguments
Ontological arguments are a type of [[Philosophy|philosophical]] argument that attempts to prove the existence of [[God]] or a higher power through the use of [[Ontology|ontological]] reasoning.

## Overview
The ontological argument is based on the idea that the concept of God implies God's existence. This argument was first proposed by [[Anselm of Canterbury|Anselm]] in the 11th century and has been debated by philosophers and theologians for centuries. The argument is often formulated as follows: God is defined as a being than which no greater can be conceived; such a being must exist, because if it did not, it would not be the greatest conceivable being.

## Concept Map
The ontological argument can be broken down into several key concepts:
* [[Existence|Existence]]: the state of being real or actual
* [[Necessity|Necessity]]: the idea that something must be the case
* [[God|God]]: the concept of a higher power or supreme being
* [[Ontology|Ontology]]: the branch of philosophy that deals with the nature of existence
* [[Modal Logic|Modal logic]]: a system of logic that deals with notions of possibility and necessity
The ontological argument relies on the idea that the concept of God implies God's existence, and that this existence is necessary and not contingent. This argument has been subject to various criticisms and challenges, including those from [[Immanuel Kant|Immanuel Kant]] and [[Bertrand Russell|Bertrand Russell]].
