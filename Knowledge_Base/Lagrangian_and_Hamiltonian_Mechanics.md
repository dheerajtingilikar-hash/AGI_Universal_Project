# Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Mechanics
Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics are two fundamental formulations of [[Classical Mechanics]], which describe the motion of objects in terms of [[Energy]] and [[Momentum]].

## Overview
Lagrangian mechanics, developed by [[Joseph-Louis Lagrange]], is based on the [[Lagrangian]] function, which is the difference between the [[Kinetic Energy]] and [[Potential Energy]] of a system. The [[Euler-Lagrange Equation]] is derived from the Lagrangian function and is used to determine the [[Equation of Motion]] of a system. Hamiltonian mechanics, developed by [[William Rowan Hamilton]], is based on the [[Hamiltonian]] function, which is the total energy of a system. The [[Hamiltonian Equations]] are derived from the Hamiltonian function and are used to determine the equation of motion of a system.

## Key Equations
* [[Lagrangian]]: L = T - U
* [[Euler-Lagrange Equation]]: ∂L/∂q - d(∂L/∂q')/dt = 0
* [[Hamiltonian]]: H = T + U
* [[Hamiltonian Equations]]: dq/dt = ∂H/∂p, dp/dt = -∂H/∂q
