# Pulmonology: Obstructive vs Restrictive Lung Disease Criteria

## Overview

Obstructive and restrictive lung diseases are two primary categories of pulmonary disorders that affect lung function. The distinction between these two categories is crucial for diagnosis and treatment.

### Definition

- **Obstructive Lung Disease**: Characterized by airway obstruction, making it difficult for air to flow out of the lungs. Examples include chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and asthma.
- **Restrictive Lung Disease**: Characterized by reduced lung capacity, making it difficult for air to enter the lungs. Examples include pulmonary fibrosis and sarcoidosis.

### Criteria for Diagnosis

#### Obstructive Lung Disease Criteria

- **Forced Expiratory Volume (FEV1)**: <80% of predicted value
- **Forced Vital Capacity (FVC)**: Normal or increased
- **FEV1/FVC Ratio**: <0.7
- **Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF)**: <80% of predicted value

#### Restrictive Lung Disease Criteria

- **Forced Vital Capacity (FVC)**: <80% of predicted value
- **Forced Expiratory Volume (FEV1)**: Normal or increased
- **FEV1/FVC Ratio**: >0.7
- **Total Lung Capacity (TLC)**: <80% of predicted value
