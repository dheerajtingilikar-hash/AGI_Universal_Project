# CRISPR-Cas9 Gene Editing
## Overview
CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing is a [[Biotechnology]] tool that allows for precise modifications to the [[DNA]] sequence of an organism. This technique utilizes a [[Bacteria|bacterial]] defense mechanism to target and edit specific [[Gene|genes]]. The CRISPR-Cas9 system consists of two main components: the [[CRISPR]] array and the [[Cas9]] enzyme. The CRISPR array is a sequence of [[Nucleotide|nucleotides]] that is complementary to the target [[DNA]] sequence, while the Cas9 enzyme is responsible for cutting the DNA at the targeted location.

## Concept Map
The CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing process can be broken down into the following steps:
1. **Target identification**: A specific [[Gene|gene]] or [[DNA]] sequence is identified as the target for editing.
2. **Guide RNA (gRNA) design**: A [[RNA|rRNA]] molecule, known as the guide RNA, is designed to be complementary to the target [[DNA]] sequence.
3. **Cas9 enzyme activation**: The Cas9 enzyme is activated and binds to the guide RNA.
4. **Target recognition**: The Cas9-gRNA complex recognizes and binds to the target [[DNA]] sequence.
5. **DNA cleavage**: The Cas9 enzyme cuts the [[DNA]] at the targeted location, creating a [[Double-strand break|double-strand break]].
6. **Repair mechanisms**: The cell's natural [[DNA repair|DNA repair mechanisms]] are activated to repair the double-strand break.
7. **Gene editing**: The [[DNA]] sequence can be modified by introducing a new [[Nucleotide|nucleotide]] sequence or by disrupting the existing sequence.
