# Metabolic Pathways (Krebs Cycle)
## Overview
The [[Krebs Cycle]], also known as the citric acid cycle or tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, is a key process by which cells generate energy. It takes place in the [[Mitochondria]] and is a crucial part of [[Cellular Respiration]]. The Krebs Cycle is an aerobic process, meaning it requires [[Oxygen]] to proceed. It is a cyclic process, where [[Acetyl-CoA]] is converted into [[Carbon Dioxide]] and energy in the form of [[ATP]], [[NADH]], and [[FADH2]].

## Concept Map
The Krebs Cycle can be divided into eight distinct steps, each catalyzed by a specific [[Enzyme]]:
1. **Citrate Synthase**: [[Citrate]] is formed from [[Acetyl-CoA]] and [[Oxaloacetate]].
2. **Aconitase**: [[Citrate]] is converted into [[Isocitrate]].
3. **Isocitrate Dehydrogenase**: [[Isocitrate]] is converted into [[Alpha-Ketoglutarate]], producing [[NADH]].
4. **Alpha-Ketoglutarate Dehydrogenase**: [[Alpha-Ketoglutarate]] is converted into [[Succinyl-CoA]], producing [[NADH]].
5. **Succinyl-CoA Synthetase**: [[Succinyl-CoA]] is converted into [[Succinate]], producing [[ATP]].
6. **Succinate Dehydrogenase**: [[Succinate]] is converted into [[Fumarate]], producing [[FADH2]].
7. **Fumarase**: [[Fumarate]] is converted into [[Malate]].
8. **Malate Dehydrogenase**: [[Malate]] is converted into [[Oxaloacetate]], producing [[NADH]].

The Krebs Cycle is closely linked to other metabolic processes, including [[Glycolysis]] and the [[Electron Transport Chain]]. It plays a critical role in the production of energy for the cell and is essential for the proper functioning of [[Aerobic Organisms]].
