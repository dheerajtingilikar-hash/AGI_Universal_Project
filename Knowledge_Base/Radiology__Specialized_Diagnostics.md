# Radiology & Specialized Diagnostics

## Overview

Radiology and specialized diagnostics refer to the use of medical imaging technologies and laboratory tests to diagnose and manage various medical conditions. This field encompasses a range of modalities, including X-ray, computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), positron emission tomography (PET), ultrasound, and nuclear medicine.

## Modalities

- [[X-ray]]: uses ionizing radiation to produce images of internal structures.
- [[Computed Tomography (CT)]]: uses X-rays and computer algorithms to produce cross-sectional images.
- [[Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)]]: uses magnetic fields and radio waves to produce detailed images of internal structures.
- [[Positron Emission Tomography (PET)]]: uses small amounts of radioactive material to produce images of metabolic activity.
- [[Ultrasound]]: uses high-frequency sound waves to produce images of internal structures.
- [[Nuclear Medicine]]: uses small amounts of radioactive material to produce images of internal structures.

## Techniques

- [[Contrast-Enhanced Imaging]]: uses contrast agents to enhance image quality.
- [[Functional Imaging]]: uses imaging modalities to assess organ function.
- [[Molecular Imaging]]: uses imaging modalities to visualize molecular processes.

## Applications

- [[Cardiovascular Imaging]]: uses imaging modalities to diagnose and manage cardiovascular diseases.
- [[Neuroimaging]]: uses imaging modalities to diagnose and manage neurological disorders.
- [[Musculoskeletal Imaging]]: uses imaging modalities to diagnose and manage musculoskeletal disorders.
- [[Pulmonary Imaging]]: uses imaging modalities to diagnose and manage pulmonary diseases.
