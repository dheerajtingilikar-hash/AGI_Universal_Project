# Linguistics
Linguistics is the scientific study of [[Language]], including its structure, properties, and usage.

## Overview
Linguistics is an interdisciplinary field that draws on [[Anthropology]], [[Computer Science]], [[Philosophy]], and [[Psychology]] to understand the complex nature of human language. It encompasses various subfields, including [[Phonetics]], [[Phonology]], [[Morphology]], [[Syntax]], and [[Semantics]]. Linguists examine language in its social and cultural context, exploring how it is acquired, used, and varied across different populations and communities.

## Concept Map
* [[Language Acquisition]]: the process by which humans acquire the ability to perceive and comprehend language
* [[Language Variation]]: the study of differences in language use across social, regional, and cultural contexts
* [[Linguistic Relativity]]: the hypothesis that language influences thought and perception
* [[Pragmatics]]: the study of how language is used in social contexts to convey meaning and achieve communication goals
* [[Sociolinguistics]]: the study of the relationship between language and society, including language policy, language planning, and language ideology

## Subfields
* [[Phonetics]]: the study of the physical properties of speech sounds
* [[Phonology]]: the study of the sound system of language
* [[Morphology]]: the study of the internal structure of words and how they are formed
* [[Syntax]]: the study of how words are combined to form sentences
* [[Semantics]]: the study of meaning in language

## Related Fields
* [[Anthropology]]: the study of human cultures and societies
* [[Computer Science]]: the study of computational systems and algorithms
* [[Philosophy]]: the study of fundamental questions about existence, knowledge, and reality
* [[Psychology]]: the study of human behavior and mental processes

## Key Theorists
* [[Noam Chomsky]]: known for his work on generative grammar and the theory of [[Universal Grammar]]
* [[Ferdinand de Saussure]]: known for his work on structural linguistics and the concept of [[Signifier]] and [[Signified]]
* [[William Labov]]: known for his work on language variation and change, and the concept of [[Sociolinguistics]]
