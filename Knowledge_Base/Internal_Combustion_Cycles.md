# Internal Combustion Cycles
Internal combustion cycles refer to the process by which a combination of [[Air]] and [[Fuel]] is ignited inside a [[Combustion Chamber]], resulting in the generation of [[Mechanical Energy]]. This process is a crucial component of various types of [[Internal Combustion Engine|Internal Combustion Engines]], including [[Spark-Ignition Engine|Spark-Ignition Engines]] and [[Compression-Ignition Engine|Compression-Ignition Engines]].

## Overview
Internal combustion cycles involve a series of processes, including [[Intake Stroke|Intake]], [[Compression Stroke|Compression]], [[Power Stroke|Power]], and [[Exhaust Stroke|Exhaust]]. The [[Four-Stroke Cycle|Four-Stroke Cycle]] is a common type of internal combustion cycle, which consists of four distinct strokes: intake, compression, power, and exhaust. Another type of internal combustion cycle is the [[Two-Stroke Cycle|Two-Stroke Cycle]], which combines the intake and exhaust strokes into a single stroke.
