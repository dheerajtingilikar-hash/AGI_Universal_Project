# Radiology: CT vs MRI Clinical Indications

## Overview

Radiology is a critical component of modern healthcare, and the choice between computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans depends on various factors, including the clinical indication, patient condition, and availability of equipment.

### CT Scan

- **Definition**: CT scans use X-rays and computer technology to produce cross-sectional images of the body.
- **Indications**:
  - **Emergency situations**: Head trauma, acute abdominal pain, and pulmonary embolism.
  - **Cancer diagnosis**: Lung, liver, and pancreatic cancer.
  - **Vascular diseases**: Aortic aneurysm, carotid artery disease.
  - **Bone and joint disorders**: Osteoporosis, joint inflammation.
- **Advantages**: Fast imaging, high spatial resolution, and low cost.
- **Disadvantages**: Radiation exposure, contrast allergy risk.

### MRI Scan

- **Definition**: MRI uses a strong magnetic field and radio waves to produce detailed images of the body.
- **Indications**:
  - **Soft tissue evaluation**: Musculoskeletal disorders, neurological conditions (e.g., multiple sclerosis).
  - **Joint and spine disorders**: Degenerative joint disease, spinal cord injuries.
  - **Brain and nervous system disorders**: Stroke, brain tumors.
  - **Pregnancy and pediatric imaging**: Fetal development, pediatric trauma.
- **Advantages**: No radiation exposure, high soft tissue contrast.
- **Disadvantages**: Claustrophobia, long examination time, high cost.
