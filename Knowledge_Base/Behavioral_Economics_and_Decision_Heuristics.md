# Behavioral Economics and Decision Heuristics

## Overview

Behavioral economics is an interdisciplinary field that combines insights from psychology, economics, and other social sciences to understand how people make decisions in the face of uncertainty and incomplete information. It challenges the traditional assumptions of neoclassical economics, which assumes that individuals make rational choices based on complete information.

Behavioral economics has identified several decision heuristics that people use to simplify complex decision-making processes. These heuristics can lead to systematic biases and errors in decision-making.

## Concept Map

### Decision Heuristics

- [[Loss Aversion]]: The tendency to prefer avoiding losses to acquiring gains.
- [[Framing Effect]]: The way in which information is presented can influence decision-making.
- [[Anchoring]]: The tendency to rely too heavily on the first piece of information encountered when making a decision.
- [[Availability Heuristic]]: The tendency to overestimate the importance of information that is readily available.
- [[Representative Bias]]: The tendency to judge the likelihood of an event based on how closely it resembles a typical case.
- [[Sunk Cost Fallacy]]: The tendency to continue investing in a decision because of the resources already committed, rather than considering the potential benefits and costs of continuing.

### Cognitive Biases

- [[Confirmation Bias]]: The tendency to seek out information that confirms one's existing beliefs.
- [[Hindsight Bias]]: The tendency to believe, after an event has occurred, that it was predictable.
- [[Illusion of Control]]: The tendency to believe that one has more control over events than one actually does.
- [[Optimism Bias]]: The tendency to overestimate the likelihood of positive outcomes.

### Behavioral Economics Theories

- [[Prospect Theory]]: A theory of decision-making under uncertainty that describes how people make choices based on the potential gains and losses.
- [[Dual-Process Theory]]: A theory that describes how people use two different systems to make decisions: a fast, intuitive system and a slow, deliberative system.

### Applications

- [[Public Policy]]: Behavioral economics has been used to design policies that take into account the biases and heuristics that people use when making decisions.
- [[Marketing]]: Understanding how people make decisions can help marketers design more effective advertising and product design.
- [[Financial Decision-Making]]: Behavioral economics has been used to design financial products and services that take into account the biases and heuristics that people use when making financial decisions.
