# Rare Earth Mineral Supply Chains

## Overview

Rare earth minerals (REMs) are a group of 17 elements with unique properties used in high-tech applications. The global demand for REMs has increased significantly due to their use in renewable energy technologies, electronics, and advanced ceramics. However, the supply chain of REMs is complex and vulnerable to disruptions.

### Key Statistics

- Global REM production: 140,000 metric tons (2020)
- China: 70% of global REM production (2020)
- REM reserves: 140 million metric tons (estimated)
- REM recycling rate: 1% (2020)

## Concept Map

### Supply Chain Components

- **Mining**: Extraction of REMs from primary sources (ores)
- **Processing**: Separation and purification of REMs from ores
- **Refining**: Final processing of REMs into high-purity products
- **Manufacturing**: Production of REM-containing products (e.g., magnets, catalysts)
- **Recycling**: Collection and processing of REM-containing waste materials

### Supply Chain Risks

- **Geopolitical risks**: Concentration of production in China, potential trade disruptions
- **Environmental risks**: Pollution from mining and processing operations
- **Economic risks**: Fluctuations in demand and prices, potential for supply chain disruptions

### Alternative Supply Chains

- **Recycling**: Closed-loop recycling of REM-containing products
- **Substitution**: Development of alternative materials and technologies
- **New mining projects**: Exploration and development of new REM deposits
