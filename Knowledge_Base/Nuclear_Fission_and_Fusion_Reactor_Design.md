# Nuclear Fission and Fusion Reactor Design

## Overview

Nuclear fission and fusion reactor design involves the development of systems that harness the energy released from nuclear reactions to generate electricity. Fission reactors rely on the splitting of heavy atomic nuclei, typically uranium-235, to release energy, whereas fusion reactors aim to achieve energy release through the combination of light atomic nuclei, such as hydrogen isotopes.

### Fission Reactor Design

Fission reactors employ a heat exchanger to convert the thermal energy generated by nuclear fission into steam, which drives a turbine to produce electricity. The primary components of a fission reactor include:

- **Fuel**: Typically enriched uranium (U-235) or other fissile materials.
- **Moderator**: A substance that slows down neutrons to increase the likelihood of fission, such as water or heavy water.
- **Control Rods**: Adjustable rods made of neutron-absorbing materials that regulate the reaction rate.
- **Coolant**: A substance that removes heat from the reactor core, such as water or gas.

### Fusion Reactor Design

Fusion reactors aim to achieve controlled nuclear fusion, where two or more atomic nuclei combine to form a single, heavier nucleus. This process releases a significant amount of energy. The primary components of a fusion reactor include:

- **Plasma**: A high-energy state of matter where atomic nuclei are ionized and highly energetic.
- **Magnetic Confinement**: A system of magnetic fields that contains and stabilizes the plasma.
- **Breeding Blanket**: A material that captures and converts the energy released from fusion reactions into a usable form.
