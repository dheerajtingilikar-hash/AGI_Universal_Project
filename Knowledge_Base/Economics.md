# Economics
Economics is the social science that studies how individuals, businesses, governments, and societies allocate resources to meet their unlimited wants and needs.

## Overview
Economics is divided into two main branches: [[Microeconomics]] and [[Macroeconomics]]. [[Microeconomics]] focuses on the behavior and decision-making of individual economic units, such as households and firms, while [[Macroeconomics]] examines the economy as a whole, including issues like [[Inflation]], [[Unemployment]], and [[Economic Growth]].

## Concept Map
The key concepts in economics are interconnected and can be represented as follows:
- [[Supply and Demand]] determine [[Market Equilibrium]]
- [[Opportunity Cost]] is a fundamental concept in understanding [[Scarcity]]
- [[Comparative Advantage]] explains why countries engage in [[International Trade]]
- [[Fiscal Policy]] and [[Monetary Policy]] are used to stabilize the economy
- [[Economic Systems]], such as [[Capitalism]] and [[Socialism]], shape the organization of economic activity

## Key Terms
- [[Economic Indicator]]: a statistic used to measure the performance of an economy
- [[Gross Domestic Product (GDP)]]: the total value of goods and services produced within a country's borders
- [[Inflation Rate]]: the rate at which prices for goods and services are rising
- [[Unemployment Rate]]: the percentage of the labor force that is currently unemployed

## Related Fields
- [[Finance]]: the study of how individuals, businesses, and governments raise and allocate capital
- [[Accounting]]: the process of recording, classifying, and reporting financial information
- [[Business Administration]]: the study of how organizations are managed and operated
